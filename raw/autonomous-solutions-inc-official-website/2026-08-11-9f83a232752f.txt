Frequently Asked Questions
What does OEM-agnostic autonomy mean?
An OEM-agnostic autonomy platform can operate across multiple vehicle brands and types rather than locking customers into a single manufacturer ecosystem. This gives organizations greater flexibility when scaling or modernizing fleets over time. ASI's Mobius platform works with 100+ vehicle types — regardless of who built them.
Why is mixed-fleet orchestration important?
Most real-world operations include a combination of autonomous vehicles, human-operated vehicles, legacy equipment, and multiple vehicle manufacturers. OEM-proprietary platforms are typically designed to manage their own vehicles — not a heterogeneous fleet. Mixed-fleet orchestration allows all of these systems to operate together within a unified operational environment. The ability to do it well and at scale reflects genuine depth of industrial experience.
Why does vendor lock-in matter in autonomy?
Autonomous vehicle technology is evolving rapidly. If your orchestration platform only works with one OEM's hardware, you're locked into that vendor's innovation pace. When a better vehicle becomes available for your application, a closed platform means you can't adopt it without replacing your entire software infrastructure. Open architecture platforms like Mobius help organizations maintain greater control over their fleet strategy and future integrations.
Can ASI integrate with existing fleet management systems?
Yes. ASI supports third-party fleet management system integration and can be retrofitted onto existing fleets, helping organizations modernize operations without replacing entire vehicles or ecosystems. Mobius is designed to work with your current infrastructure — not replace it.
