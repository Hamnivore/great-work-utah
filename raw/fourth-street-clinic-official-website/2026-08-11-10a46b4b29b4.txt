OUR MISSION
Fourth Street Clinic delivers accessible, comprehensive quality care to individuals facing homelessness for improved health and lasting impact.​
For many Utahns experiencing homelessness, Fourth Street Clinic is their first and only chance at a diagnosis and ongoing treatment. By increasing access to primary care, Fourth Street Clinic is a major partner in ending homelessness, promoting community health and achieving across-the-board health care savings.

Nora Trinidad-Scholle
Nora Trinidad-Scholle is a dynamic and innovative nonprofit leader with more than two decades of experience in public service administration, operations, and community impact. Her career has been defined by transformative leadership, strategic growth, and an unwavering commitment to equity and social change.
Nora currently serves as the Chief Operating Officer at Girl Scouts of Utah, where she brings her signature blend of operational excellence and movement-building expertise to one of the state’s most enduring youth-serving organizations. She joined the council from the Larry H. & Gail Miller Family Foundation—one of Utah’s premier philanthropic institutions—where she helped shape the foundation’s evolution into one of the state’s fastest-growing and most impactful funders.
Previously, Nora served as Chief Operating Officer at YWCA Utah, a 116-year-old organization dedicated to eliminating racism and empowering women. There, she led a broad portfolio that included organizational operations, external relations, infrastructure, risk management, and strategic planning, all while centering equity and mission alignment in every facet of her work.
A passionate community builder and systems thinker, Nora is known for weaving together strategy, creativity, and collaboration to drive measurable outcomes. She brings a sharp business mind, a growth mindset, and a deep belief that community-centered, justice-driven leadership can transform organizations and the lives they touch.
A dedicated advocate, Nora currently serves on the board of the Fourth Street Clinic, which provides vital healthcare to individuals experiencing homelessness. She has also served on the boards of Planned Parenthood Association of Utah, the Washington Heights Neighborhood Association (WI), and the Washington Park Partners Community Concert Series. In 2021, she was recognized as one of Utah Business Magazine’s 30 Women to Watch.
Originally from the Midwest, Nora made her home in the Mountain West over a decade ago and never looked back. She’s an avid skier (and occasional coach), a novice carpenter, and an urban homesteader. She lives in Salt Lake City with her partner Mike, their blended family of four kids, and a menagerie of pets—including a lively flock of chickens named Lisa, Nila, Zoomies, Punky, Angelica, and Zelda.

Osman Sanyer, MD
Dr. Osman Sanyer is a family physician who cared for patients in Salt Lake City for nearly forty years. In 1986, Dr. Sanyer was one of the original group of providers who established the Salt Lake Community Health Centers, where he served as medical director from 1990-1993. In that role, he was involved with the administration of the 4 th Street Clinic during the clinic’s early years of existence.
From 1997 through his retirement in 2024, Dr. Sanyer was a full-time faculty member in the University of Utah Department of Family & Preventive Medicine. He served as the residency program director or associate director for seventeen years during that time period. Dr. Sanyer’s primary areas of clinical and academic interest focused on obstetrics and maternal-child health for the first portion of his career at the University and behavioral health in primary care in his later career. Since retiring, Dr. Sanyer continues to engage the community through volunteer and mentoring activities. Dr. Sanyer received his M.D. from the University of Wisconsin-Madison in 1983 and completed his family medicine residency at the University of Rochester in 1986.

Lisa Michele Church
Lisa-Michele Church’s professional background includes more than 30 years of public and private service as an attorney, historian, and community activist. She served in the Executive Cabinet for two Utah governors where she advocated for issues of social justice from helping individual families negotiate the challenges of mental illness to advocating at a national level for more effective substance abuse prevention strategies during Utah’s methamphetamine epidemic. In her law practice she served as Vice President/General Counsel at a Fortune 500 company where she was instrumental in negotiating the venue agreements for the 2002 Salt Lake City Winter Olympic Games.
As Executive Director of Utah’s Department of Human Services, Ms. Church addressed child protection, juvenile justice, mental illness, drug addiction, services for people with disabilities, elder abuse, and child support collection. Subsequently, as Court Administrator for Utah’s juvenile court system, she was able to continue her advocacy for youth and families across courts statewide. She worked with judges and court staff to prioritize the needs of underserved youth such as girls in the juvenile justice system and teenagers aging out of foster care without family attachments.
She served on the Utah State Board of Higher Education (formerly Board of Regents) from 2019-2023 and chaired the board during 2022-2023. She believes strongly in the power of education to change the trajectories of entire families and works toward inclusivity and opportunity for all.
Lisa-Michele is a published historian by avocation and passion. Having grown up in the California and Utah, she is interested in vernacular architecture, especially early 20 th century mom-and-pop businesses such as motels, cafes, and grocery stores. She speaks often to community groups on topics of tourism, iconic neon signs, vintage apartment buildings, and road-building throughout Utah. Her publications include Vintage Signs of Utah – Volume Two (Relentless History, 2024), Vintage Signs of Utah – Volume One (Relentless History, 2022); Historic Apartment Buildings of Salt Lake City (Relentless History, 2019); “Historic Salt Lake City Apartments of the Early Twentieth Century” ( Utah Historical Quarterly , Vol. 84, Num.1, 2016); and “Early Roadside Motels and Motor Courts of St. George” ( Utah Historical Quarterly, Vol. 80, Num. 1, 2012). Her presentations include Isolation to Inundation: Tourism History in Southern Utah,(Smithsonian Symposium, 2023); Red Rocks and Race Cars: Charles Bigelow and the Arrowhead Highway; Handmade Adobe Brick Homes of Panguitch, Utah; Women-Owned Beauty Shops in Rural Utah; From the Land No One Wanted to the Land Everyone Wanted to See.
