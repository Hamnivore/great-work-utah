Executive
Summary

SafeUT was developed in response to Utah’s
unacceptably high youth suicide rate—the leading
cause of death for people aged 10-24—and
increasing school safety threats. The program was
created when Utah State Senator Daniel Thatcher
alerted Utah Attorney General Sean Reyes to the
critical need for a student crisis and tip line and
introduced legislation, co-sponsored by Utah
State Representative Steve Eliason, in 2014. The
first School Safety Tip Line bill created a statewide
commission, chaired by the Utah Attorney General’s
Office, with representation from statewide partners
in public and higher education, behavioral health,
law enforcement, elected officials, and members of
the general public.

Launched in 2016, the SafeUT app was
redeveloped by the University of Utah to provide
students, parents, guardians, and educators
with a confidential way to connect with licensed
counselors. SafeUT counselors hold master’s
degrees in mental health and are employed by
the Huntsman Mental Health Institute at University
of Utah Health. Today, SafeUT’s service is also
available as an app on Chromebooks and other

computers via our website, SafeUT.org, where,
where you can access immediate help from SafeUT
counselors, submit tips, or tap into helpful resources.

SafeUT is nationally recognized for its effectiveness
in saving lives and de-escalating potential school
threats using a behavioral health-first approach.
People submit private tips to SafeUT when they
have a concern about the safety of someone else
or their school. SafeUT regularly gets tips about
bullying, concerns about someone who has thoughts
of self-harm or thoughts of suicide, concerns about
someone hurting others, or potential school safety
threats. A SafeUT counselor reviews the safety
concern and passes it to the school. Schools use
the information SafeUT provides and may follow up
with the tip if there’s a safety concern. For high-risk
situations, police may need to get involved to keep
the school safe.

SafeUT National Guard was launched in 2019,
followed by SafeUT Frontline in 2020.

Whether you start a chat or submit a tip, SafeUT
counselors are ready to help 24/7, 365 days a
year for free.

“SafeUT gives people a fighting chance to
survive their darkest moments. Over a thousand
Utahns are alive today because of SafeUT—
almost 350 in the last year alone. You probably
know some of them. Thank you to the pioneers
who joined forces to build this initiative, the
gifted teams that make it work, and to each
person who reached out when it mattered.
All of our lives are richer because of you.”
Ric Cantrell
SafeUT Commission Chair

How SafeUT Works

Start a Chat
Have a live conversation with a professional mental health counselor, 24/7 at no cost.

Chat
Workflow
Tap the “Start a
Chat” button to
get started.

A SafeUT counselor
will receive and
respond to the chat.

Crisis or concern is
resolved after conversation
with a SafeUT counselor.

If additional support
is needed, the SafeUT
counselor will collaborate
with appropriate
community partner(s).

Submit a Tip
To keep schools safe, submit a confidential tip when you have a
concern about the safety of someone else or your school. Describe
what you’re concerned about and include photos if you’d like.

Tip
Workflow
Tap the “Submit
a Tip” button
to voice your
concern.

A SafeUT counselor
will read the tip
immediately to see
if it’s an emergency
and ask follow-up
questions as needed.

If it’s not an emergency,
the SafeUT counselor will
pass the tip along so that
a school leader can follow
up during school hours and
respond according to their
school safety protocol.

For high-risk situations,
the SafeUT counselor will
notify the school in real
time. Police may need
to get involved to keep
schools safe.

Start a Call
If you prefer to talk to someone
instead of chatting, you can use the
call feature to talk to a certified crisis
worker with the Utah Crisis Line.
This feature is supported by the same
staff who answer the 988 Suicide
and Crisis Lifeline calls.

View Existing
Chats & Tips
The SafeUT app on your personal
device is password protected to
provide additional privacy and allows
you to view your chats and tips for
future reference.

Keeping
Schools Safe
Preventing school violence starts with recognizing
that there is no single profile of a student who
may plan a school attack. They are someone’s
child, someone’s friend, and often, someone in
crisis. Students who plan such attacks may have
grievances, but they frequently possess a secondary
motive, such as experiencing social stressors or
feeling suicidal.

According to the U.S. Secret Service’s National
Threat Assessment Center (NTAC), almost all averted
school attacks reveal intervention opportunities
before violence occurs. Based on their 2021 findings,
the NTAC outlined important considerations for
creating policies for multidisciplinary school threat
assessment teams.

SafeUT is part of a broader effort to ensure Utah
schools stay safe. Whenever SafeUT gets a possible
threat to a school or campus, the SafeUT counselor
notifies the school right away and partners with law
enforcement as needed to keep students safe.

Tip

Know the Signs
US Department of Homeland
Security research shows that
if we “know the signs” of
violence, we can prevent it.
Critical warning signs of planned
attacks or hurting others include:

• Suddenly withdrawing from
friends, family, and activities
(including online via
social media)

• Bullying, especially if targeted
towards differences in race,
religion, gender, or sexual
orientation

• Excessive irritability, lack
of patience, or becoming
angry quickly

• Experiencing chronic loneliness
or social isolation

• Expressing persistent thoughts
of harming themselves or
someone else

• Making direct threats toward
a place, another person,
or themselves

• Bragging about access to
guns or weapons

• Recruiting accomplices for
an attack

• Directly expressing a threat
as a plan

Students
Students are often the first to notice when
something feels off with a classmate. If a peer
shows concerning, violent, or hate-filled behavior
in-person or online, students can use SafeUT’s
“Submit a Tip” feature to report it confidentially.
Speaking up can protect lives and get someone
the help they need.

Schools
Educators, administrators, and school resource
officers play a crucial role in violence protection.
Concerning behavior should prompt immediate
assessment—not just punishment. Simply removing
a student from school doesn’t eliminate risk.
If something feels wrong, staff can confidentially
report it through SafeUT.

Families
Parents and guardians should talk with their child
about what’s happening at school and listen
closely to concerns. If a student is uncomfortable
using SafeUT themselves, adults are strongly
encouraged to help them submit a tip. Family
awareness and action can be essential to
preventing harm.

Communities
Stopping school violence requires teamwork.
Students, families, and schools must share
information and act on warning signs. Research
shows most school attacks are driven by personal
grievances, and many involved easy access to
firearms. With early intervention, these tragedies
are preventable.

A Lifeline for
the Frontline
The SafeUT Frontline app launched in 2020 to
provide a safe line for those who work in health
care, fire/EMS, law enforcement, corrections,
and their families. As the ongoing need for mental
health support among Utah’s frontline workers
persists, public safety dispatch—whose work
involves listening to traumatic calls and making
high-stakes decisions—has been added as a
dedicated group to SafeUT Frontline’s service.

SafeUT Frontline aids Utah’s frontline workers
by linking them to licensed mental health
professionals—who are trained to understand
their unique challenges—for confidential support.

What’s Next for SafeUT
January 2026 will mark 10 years since SafeUT
first began supporting the mental health of Utah
students and working to keep schools safe. Over
the past decade, thousands of students, parents,
and educators have turned to SafeUT for trusted
support, valuable resources, and help in moments
of crisis.

Through ongoing partnerships with Utah schools
and communities, SafeUT has grown and adapted
to meet the needs of those it serves. Its impact can

be seen not only in the number of lives touched,
but also in the work it continues to do—providing
accessible mental health support and playing an
impactful role in school safety year after year.

As SafeUT approaches its decade milestone, its
commitment to Utah remains as strong as ever.
The work ahead will focus on expanding resources,
strengthening school and community partnerships,
and continuing to prioritize safety and well-being
for every student in Utah.
