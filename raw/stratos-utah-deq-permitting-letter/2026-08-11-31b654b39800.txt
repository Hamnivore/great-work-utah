Dear Commissioners,

The Utah Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) is providing this correspondence to clarify
the permitting responsibilities and regulatory oversight required for the proposed data center and
supporting power plant project in Box Elder County. Our objective is to ensure the Commission
understands the rigorous standards for any Utah facility that discharges into waters of the state,
creates emissions into Utah’s airshed, or requires a public drinking water system.

Air Quality Permitting (Division of Air Quality)

The Utah Division of Air Quality (DAQ) implements the Utah Air Conservation Act, and has
delegated authority from the U.S. EPA to implement the federal Clean Air Act. Before any
construction can begin, the developer must obtain an Approval Order (AO) that evaluates
projected air pollutant emissions and mandates specific control Techniques.

An Approval Order is granted if the applicant demonstrates the following:

   ●​ Best Available Control Technology (BACT): Use of the highest standard of technology to
      minimize emissions.
   ●​ NAAQS Compliance: Demonstration through air pollutant dispersion modeling (using
      local Box Elder meteorology) that emissions will not violate National Ambient Air Quality
      Standards.
   ●​ New Source Performance Standards (NSPS): Compliance with federal standards for all
      selected emission sources.
   ●​ Prevention of Significant Deterioration (PSD): Ensuring that local air quality is protected
      from significant degradation.

Drinking Water Regulatory Oversight (Division of Drinking Water)

The Utah Division of Drinking Water (DDW) has delegated authority from the U.S. EPA to
implement the federal Safe Drinking Water Act. Based on current project understanding, DDW
assumes the data center and power plant will operate as separate Public Water Systems (PWS),
sourcing water from independent groundwater wells.

Infrastructure & Operations
   ●​ Separation of Systems: Drinking water systems must be physically separate from water
       used for cooling, fire protection, or power generation.
   ●​ Source Approval: Wells must be designed to DDW standards, including rigorous source
       protection and potential treatment depending on water quality.
   ●​ Facilities: Systems must meet minimum storage and pressure requirements, likely
       necessitating dedicated tanks and pump stations.
   ●​ Certified Operators: Each system must be managed by a Certified Water Operator
       responsible for all testing, including monthly bacteria sampling.
   ●​ Reporting: Systems are subject to annual fees, regular inspections, and continuous
       reporting to ensure public health safety.


Water Quality Permitting (Division of Water Quality)

The Division of Water Quality (DWQ) is responsible for protecting the waters of the state
under the Utah Water Quality Act and delegated authority from the U.S. EPA to implement the
federal Clean Water Act. Permits are required for all surface water and groundwater discharges to
waters of the state. These permits include a rigorous public notice process to ensure community
awareness and involvement.

   ●​ Discharge Permits: This is the primary mechanism for pollution prevention. Permits are
      required for all discharges, whether to surface waters or ground waters.
   ●​ Protection of Use: The Division’s processes ensure that discharges do not harm the
      designated uses of state waters.


We are available to provide additional information on our regulatory oversight and these technical
