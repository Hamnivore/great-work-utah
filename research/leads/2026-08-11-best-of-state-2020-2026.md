# Leads: Best of State winners, 2020–2026

Harvested 2026-08-11 from the Best of State archive. **Nothing here has been verified.** Every
entry is a candidate for a `wiki/pages/` page, and every claim in the "why" column is my
summary from the award listing plus prior knowledge — it must be replaced with a real cited
source before anything ships. Treat this file as a work queue, not as knowledge.

## Source

- Index: https://www.bestofstate.org/past-winners (archive covers 2020–2025 only)
- Year pages: `https://www.bestofstate.org/past-winners?year=YYYY` for 2020–2025 — these are
  server-rendered, so the query parameter really does return different content.
- Current year: https://www.bestofstate.org/winners (2026, 474 winners)
- 2025 also offers a PDF: `2025_bos_winners.pdf` (4.5 MB), linked from the year page.

Roughly 3,200 medal-winner rows across seven years. Most winners repeat across years and across
categories, so the distinct-organization count is far lower — on the order of 700–900.

## Calibration — read this before trusting the award as evidence

Four things about this source materially change how much a Best of State medal is worth as
evidence, and all four should be recorded on any `source` page built from it.

1. **It is nomination-and-entry driven, not a survey of the field.** Someone has to enter and
   pay. So a medal is weak positive evidence that an organization exists and cared enough to
   apply; the *absence* of an organization means nothing at all. Utah's largest and most
   consequential companies are almost entirely missing from these lists.
2. **One person, David F. Pack, Ph.D., won across five to seven unrelated categories every single
   year** — Actor, Adjunct Professor, College/University Teacher, Individual Volunteer, Appointed
   Official, Elected Local Official, Recreational Coach, Volunteer Service. That pattern is only
   explicable as diligent repeat entry, and it caps how much any single medal can mean.
3. **The awards program's own vendors win categories.** Michelle Leo Events won Event Planner
   repeatedly while the winner pages link to her Best-of-State landing pages; several winners link
   to pages built specifically for the award. Note the conflict, don't infer fraud.
4. **The 2026 winners page carousel contains fabricated demo data.** Six entries — Cedarbrook
   Bistro, Wasatch Atelier, Nucleus Labs, High Lake Cellars, Granite Works, Beacon Wildlands
   Trust — repeat identically for every year 2020 through 2025 with the same medal numbers, the
   same one-line descriptions, and rotating stock photos. They do not appear in any year's actual
   winner list. **Do not ingest them.** They are placeholder content left in a site rebuild. The
   flat directory lower on each page is the real data.

Also: the archive starts at 2020, but Best of State has run since 2002. The earlier years have
since been recovered from the Wayback Machine — see
[`2026-08-11-best-of-state-2003-2019.md`](2026-08-11-best-of-state-2003-2019.md), which covers
2003 and 2005–2019 and adds four further calibration points, including the distinction between the
bronze **medal** (many per division) and the **BOSS statue** (one per division per year, selected
from that year's medalists). This file does not make that distinction; the 2026 winners it lists
are all medals.

## Ingested, second pass (2026-08-11, later)

**The award itself is now documented**, which was the blocker on every page that cites a medal:
`best-of-state-awards.md` (`Type: resource`) plus seven captured source pages — the 2025 program book
and the 2019 winners directory (both PDFs, the 2019 one recovered from the Internet Archive), the 2026
winners page, the past-winners archive, and the about, categories, and nomination pages. Three findings
that change how every downstream page should read a medal:

- **764 categories, 474 medals.** The taxonomy is fine enough to contain 215 dining categories, so a
  medal is best *entrant* in a narrow slot. Entry costs $300, or $85 in reduced-fee categories, and
  payment is step two of the submission form. Self-nomination is encouraged and judging is "based
  solely on information provided in the nomination."
- **Medal ≠ statue.** One Best of State Statue per division per year, chosen from that division's
  medalists — roughly ten a year against ~474 medals. The program's current website conflates them;
  wiki pages must say which.
- **The rebuilt official site carries fabricated winner cards.** Six featured "winner circle"
  organizations repeat identically for 2020 through 2025 with the same medal numbers and appear in no
  actual winner list. Never ingest a featured card; the flat directory lower on the page is the real
  data. The about page's three headline statistics each contradict a document the program published
  itself, and are cited for nothing.

**Track A is effectively complete.** Every live Tier 1 lead in both files now has a fact page plus
captured sources — YESCO, auticon US, Spectrum Solutions (with an FDA 510(k) record), Crumbl, Blue
Raven Solar, Mountain Heights Academy, CHOICE Humanitarian, Huntsman World Senior Games, Sistema Utah,
SafeUT, Hope Squad, Apa Sherpa Foundation, Carterra (with the Wasatch Microfluidics rename sourced),
Imagine Learning, CompHealth, Globus Relief, Orbit Irrigation Products. Outstanding: **Catherine
deVries** (no page, no recorded refusal — check whether a shard refused it and why) and the four
defunct entities held back for Wayback judgment (Linux Networx, Millenniata/M-DISC, Altiris,
Wirthlin).

**Track B item 1 is done** — see the troubled-teen cluster note in the companion file for what the

## Tier 1 — strongest leads

These are the ones I'd write first. Each is plausibly world-affecting or genuinely unusual, and
most are absent from the wiki today.

### Best Friends Animal Society / Best Friends Animal Sanctuary — Kanab
Won Attractions, Motels/Boutique Lodging, Animal Services, and Vegetarian/Vegan (2022, 2023,
2024, 2025, 2026). The claim to check: it runs the largest no-kill animal sanctuary in the
United States on land near Angel Canyon, and it originated and still drives the national no-kill
movement — a genuine case of a Utah organization changing a whole country's default practice.
Also runs the Roadhouse & Mercantile (a pet-inclusive hotel) and Angel Village Cafe.
https://bestfriends.org — **not in the wiki.**

### Western Governors University — Salt Lake City / Millcreek
Won On-line Education 2020, 2021, 2022. Founded by nineteen western governors; nonprofit,
competency-based, no credit hours, enrollment in the six figures. If the competency-based degree
model at national scale traces back to a Utah institution, this is one of the higher-breadth
items available anywhere in the corpus. https://www.wgu.edu — **not in the wiki.**

### The Other Side Academy + The Other Side Village — Salt Lake City / Orem
Won Substance Abuse Treatment, Adult Education, Vocational Training, Humanitarian Organization
(2021–2024); Joseph Grenny won Individual Humanitarian in 2025. Free two-year residential
therapeutic community for people coming out of prison and addiction, funded by businesses the
students themselves run (moving, thrift, construction), plus a tiny-home village for the
chronically homeless. Unusual funding model, unusually strong claimed outcomes — worth real
scrutiny in both directions. https://www.theothersideacademy.com — **not in the wiki.**

### The INN Between — Salt Lake City
Won Charitable/Service Organization 2021. **Verified in this session:** one of the first hospices
in America built specifically for people experiencing homelessness, opened 2015 in a former
convent, now a licensed Type II assisted living facility expanding from 63 to 80 beds, with an
approved Utah 1115 Medical Respite waiver. Publishes hard outcome numbers (readmission
reductions, per-patient cost savings) that are exactly the kind of load-bearing figure the
raw-source-capture plan wants captured verbatim.
https://tibhospice.org — FY25 annual report PDF is the best citable artifact. **Not in the wiki.**

### SafeUT — University of Utah HMHI + Utah Attorney General's Office
Won Web-based Community Resource every year 2020–2024. Statewide crisis chat and anonymous tip
app for K-12 and university students, staffed by licensed clinicians. Check the claims about
intervened suicides and averted school threats — if they hold up this is high-depth,
state-wide-breadth public health infrastructure that other states have copied.
https://safeut.org — **not in the wiki.**

### YESCO (Young Electric Sign Company) — Salt Lake City / Ogden
2026 Signage & Advertising winner. Founded 1920, still family-owned; the claim to check is that
YESCO built and maintains most of the iconic Las Vegas Strip signage including the "Welcome to
Fabulous Las Vegas" sign, Vegas Vic, and the Golden Nugget marquee. A century-old Utah company
that manufactured another state's most recognizable visual identity is a strong story.
https://www.yesco.com — **not in the wiki.**

### auticon US — Salt Lake City
Won Employer 2023. IT consultancy whose consultant workforce is exclusively autistic adults.
A genuine labor-market experiment with a real commercial business behind it, and directly
relevant to the wiki's job-matching use case. https://www.auticon.com/us — **not in the wiki.**

### Spectrum Solutions — Draper
Won Medical Innovation 2021 and 2022, Laboratory Equipment 2022. Made the FDA-authorized saliva
collection device used for COVID-19 testing at very large volume. Check the scale numbers — this
may be one of the highest-unit-count medical devices ever shipped from Utah.
https://www.spectrumsolution.com — **not in the wiki.**

### Crumbl — Lindon
Won Cookies, Bakery, Desserts, Gourmet Cookies 2020–2023 and Desserts 2026. Founded 2017 in
Logan; became one of the fastest-growing franchise systems in the United States, with a weekly
rotating menu and a social-media-native distribution model that reshaped an entire dessert
category. High breadth, contested depth. https://crumbl.com — **not in the wiki.**

### Blue Raven Solar — Orem
Won Solar Technology 2020 and 2021. Grew from a Provo startup to one of the largest residential
solar installers in the country, acquired by SunPower in 2021. Directly on-charter for the energy
domain, which the wiki already covers heavily. https://www.blueravensolar.com — **not in the wiki.**

### Mountain Heights Academy — West Jordan
Won Charter School / K-12 Online Education / Curriculum Development 2020, and again in 2026.
Formerly the Open High School of Utah; the claim to check is that it was the first US public
school to build its entire curriculum from openly licensed materials and release its own courses
back under an open license. Small enrollment, potentially large downstream breadth. Dr. DeLaina
Tonks won Administrator K-12 in 2020. https://mountainheightsacademy.org — **not in the wiki.**

### CHOICE Humanitarian — West Jordan
Won Humanitarian Organization 2020; founder James Mayfield won Individual Humanitarian the same
year. Village-led international development model developed by a University of Utah professor,
operating in several countries with an explicit goal of ending extreme poverty village by
village. https://www.choicehumanitarian.org — **not in the wiki.**

### Huntsman World Senior Games — St. George
Won Sporting Event every year 2020–2025. Claim to check: the largest annual multi-sport event in
the world for athletes over 50, drawing many thousands of competitors from dozens of countries to
a small southern Utah city each October. https://www.seniorgames.net — **not in the wiki.**

### Sistema Utah — Draper / West Valley City
Won Community Music Program, Children's Orchestra, Musical Instruments, Youth Small Ensemble
across 2020–2026. El Sistema-model free intensive orchestral training for underserved children.
The kind of long-horizon, depth-heavy intervention the charter's permanence weighting favors.
https://www.sistemautah.org — **not in the wiki.**

## Cluster leads — patterns worth a page each

These aren't organizations; they're structural observations the seven-year sweep makes visible.
Each could support a page that no single winner justifies.

1. **Utah as the direct-selling capital of the world.** USANA, doTERRA, Xyngular, Young Living,
   Nu Skin, Melaleuca (Idaho, but the same corridor). Several appear in these lists; the industry
   as a whole is one of Utah's largest and most globally consequential exports, and it is
   contested enough that the charter's "magnitude not sign" rule is exactly the right frame.
2. **Utah as a restaurant-franchise incubator.** Crumbl, Costa Vida, Kneaders, Leatherby's,
   Apollo Burger, Chip Cookies, Roxberry, Swig and Sodalicious (absent here but part of the
   pattern). A remarkable number of national franchise systems originate in a state of three
   million people.
3. **Community theater density.** Hale Centre Theatre, CenterPoint Legacy, West Valley Arts,
   Sandy Arts Guild, Odyssey Dance, Lyceum Philharmonic, Utah Conservatory of Performing Arts,
   Sistema Utah, One Voice, Gifted Music School — all winning simultaneously, every year. Check
   the per-capita theater attendance and live-performance statistics; if Utah really is an outlier
   this is a cultural fact with no page anywhere.
4. **The Utah System of Technical Colleges.** Mountainland, Davis, Bridgerland, Snow, Uintah
   Basin. The wiki has fragments (`the-hub-at-bridgerland-technical-college`,
   `business-resource-center-davis-tech-college`) but no page on the system or its placement rates.
5. **USU Extension as statewide delivery infrastructure.** Five separate programs medaled. There
   is no wiki page for Extension itself.
6. **The Attorney General's office as a program incubator.** SafeUT, Utah@EASE, the Children's
   Justice Centers, the Opioid Task Force, the Teen Titans youth committee all trace back through
   the AG's office in these lists. Worth documenting as a mechanism, with the political
   controversy attached to that office's leadership handled honestly.
7. **Rural anchors.** IE-Corp in Price, Snow College in Ephraim, Wasatch Academy in Mt. Pleasant,
   Best Friends in Kanab, Huntsman Senior Games and InfoWest in St. George, Circle Four in
   Milford, Rancho Luna Lobos in Peoa. The wiki's region coverage is thin outside the Wasatch
   Front and every one of these is a hook.

- The ~1,400 restaurant, roofing, plumbing, HVAC, dental, orthodontic, mortuary, salon, flooring,
