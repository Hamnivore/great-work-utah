# Venture tier rerank — potential and discovery, 2026-08-13

Second full pass over all 173 `Type: venture` pages. The rest of the 485-page impact ladder retained
its first-pass ratings. This pass responds to a specific failure in the original list: recognizable
incumbents were easy to defend from demonstrated scale, while unfamiliar companies with credible
upside were compressed because their fields had other pursuers.

## Rubric correction

The original ruling 4 said to divide a field's prize by its number of credible pursuers. That was too
mechanical. Competition changes the probability that one company wins; it does not reduce the
consequence if that company wins. The replacement test asks whether the page establishes a
company-specific path to disproportionate capture: a technical architecture, natural asset, data
advantage, regulatory position, manufacturing method, validated result, or deployment wedge that can
compound.

Ruling 10 now makes the complementary point explicit: fame is neither evidence nor a prior. Obscurity
does not earn a promotion, but it cannot stand in for risk. Stars are discovery picks — pages
unusually revealing or surprising relative to their pure-displacement letter — and starred entries
sort first inside each generated tier.

## Method

The coordinating rater read and scored `venture-00` first (29 pages) to calibrate the new rule. Three
subagents then rated non-overlapping batches: `venture-01` + `venture-02`, `venture-03` +
`venture-04`, and `venture-05` plus an audit of every S/A venture. Subagents wrote TSVs only and did
not edit wiki pages. Central review checked every A, every promotion, all reported boundaries, the
old hand overrides, exact manifest coverage, and a stale renamed slug.

The three old A-to-B overrides for Curza, Halia Therapeutics, and Rodatherm Energy were removed. Each
depended solely on the retired prize-division rule; on a second reading, each page establishes both a
distinct mechanism and a credible milestone.
