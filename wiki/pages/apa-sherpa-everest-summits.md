## Summary

Apa Sherpa (Lhakpa Tenzing Sherpa) is a Nepalese mountaineer who summited Mount Everest 21 times, reaching his 21st summit in May 2011 on the Eco Everest Expedition and retiring afterward on a promise to his wife to stop at that. Twenty-one successful ascents of the world's highest peak over two decades is an extreme concentration of high-altitude work.

## Impact

Twenty-one successful ascents of the world's highest peak over two decades is an extreme concentration of high-altitude work. The record mattered because Everest commercial climbing depends on Sherpa labor — fixing rope, carrying loads, guiding clients — and Apa became the public face of that repetition.

The record is **past tense**. Readers looking for who holds it now should use [NBC/AP, May 2024](nbc-kami-rita-everest-record-2024.md) (Kami Rita, 30) rather than repeat a stale superlative.

## What It Took

Apa was born in **Thame** in Nepal's Everest region, left school after his father's death, and worked as a **porter** before his first summit opportunity in **1990** ([Wikipedia reference capture](wikipedia-apa-sherpa-reference.md)). He reached the summit in all but two years from 1990 through 2011, summiting twice in 1992.

Stopping at 21 was explicit: *"Everyone says 21 is a good number. I have to make my family happy."* ([Wikipedia reference capture](wikipedia-apa-sherpa-reference.md)).

## Utah Context

Apa's family **moved to the United States in December 2006** and **lives in Draper, Utah** ([Wikipedia reference capture](wikipedia-apa-sherpa-reference.md)). That residence connects Utah to the story after retirement; the climbs themselves happened in Nepal.

He founded the [Apa Sherpa Foundation](apa-sherpa-foundation.md) (April 2009 per Wikipedia; IRS exemption 2012) to fund education in Nepal so young Sherpas have alternatives to climbing and portering — a motivation quoted on Wikipedia and aligned with, but not verbatim on, the foundation's captured site pages.
