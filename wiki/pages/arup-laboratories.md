## Summary

ARUP Laboratories — legally **Associated Regional and University Pathologists, Inc.** — is a
Salt Lake City reference laboratory that ARUP and the University of Utah Department of Pathology
describe as a **national academic reference laboratory** and **nonprofit enterprise of the
University of Utah and its Department of Pathology**
([official site](arup-laboratories-official-website.md);
[U of U pathology](arup-uofu-pathology-about-us.md)). Both the lab and the department characterize
its work as **clinical and anatomic pathology** testing, including **highly complex and unique**
and **esoteric** tests that individual hospitals typically cannot run in-house
([about blurb](arup-uofu-pathology-about-us.md);
[facilities history](arup-uofu-pathology-facilities.md)).

ARUP reports **3,000+ tests and test combinations** and **150+ academic experts and consultants**
on its own About page ([official site](arup-laboratories-official-website.md)). It is CAP-, ISO
15189-, and CLIA-certified by its own account and says it has operated for **more than 40 years**.
The department's facilities page dates the enterprise to **1984**, when pathology faculty created
it to support education, research, and service while **financially supporting the Department of
Pathology** ([facilities history](arup-uofu-pathology-facilities.md)).

For this wiki, ARUP matters as one of Utah's largest life-sciences institutions and as an unusual
ownership model: a nonprofit reference lab whose stated purpose includes returning margin to a public
university's pathology department, sitting alongside [Myriad Genetics](myriad-genetics.md) and
[BioFire Diagnostics](biofire-diagnostics.md) in the Wasatch Front diagnostics cluster.

## Impact

The depth argument rests on what reference laboratories actually do: run assays and interpret results
that community hospitals and regional labs cannot maintain in-house. ARUP's own materials and the
pathology department both frame the lab around **esoteric testing** and a **highly complex** menu
([facilities history](arup-uofu-pathology-facilities.md);
[about blurb](arup-uofu-pathology-about-us.md)). If that framing is even roughly accurate, ARUP's
work sits upstream of a large share of diagnostic decisions — the department's About page notes that
"a majority of clinical decisions are based on the results of diagnostic tests" — but that general
claim is about pathology as a field, not a measured ARUP-specific fraction.

The breadth argument is harder to pin down from the captures alone. ARUP says it serves **hospital
and health system clients** nationally ([official site](arup-laboratories-official-website.md)) and
reports **99% testing performed on-site** at what its fact sheet lists as **1 Location**
([official site](arup-laboratories-official-website.md)). The fact sheet does not define "on-site,"
and the two natural readings differ: it may mean the share of the test menu run in-house rather than
referred out, or it may be a statement about physical consolidation. This page does not choose
between them. Without a captured client count or specimen volume, the wiki cannot assert how many
institutions or patients ARUP actually reaches — only that the lab presents itself as a national
partner operating from a single listed location.

The permanence and counterfactual argument is where the ownership structure becomes interesting.
According to the department, ARUP was created in **1984** with an explicit directive to **financially
support the Department of Pathology** while testing for University of Utah Health and building a
national reference capability ([facilities history](arup-uofu-pathology-facilities.md)). That is a
deliberate bet that a nonprofit lab can generate durable operating surplus for academic medicine
rather than distributing profit to shareholders. Whether that transfer is material in dollar terms,
and whether it materially shapes hiring or research at the university, is **not established** from
these captures — the pages describe intent, not audited amounts.

One framing caveat governs everything above. All three captured sources are either ARUP's own site or
the University of Utah pathology department **that owns ARUP**. Where this page says the lab and the
department both describe something, that is not two sources agreeing; it is one institution and its
subsidiary. Nothing on this page is independent of the subject, and no registry identifier, filing,
or third-party volume figure has been pulled.

**Bet:** that coupling a high-complexity reference lab to a university pathology department creates a
compounding loop — esoteric test development, resident training, and clinical revenue feeding each
other — that a standalone commercial lab or hospital outreach program cannot replicate as cleanly.
The honest counterweight: every scale figure on this page is self-reported, no Form 990 or independent
market data has been pulled, and "nonprofit enterprise" describes governance, not proof that surplus
actually reaches the university at a level that changes academic outcomes.

## Utah Context

ARUP is headquartered on the University of Utah Health campus in Salt Lake City. The pathology
department lists it as a primary training site and affiliated organization
([about blurb](arup-uofu-pathology-about-us.md);
[facilities history](arup-uofu-pathology-facilities.md)). ARUP also contributed to campus
infrastructure — the facilities page notes that donations from ARUP helped fund the Emma Eccles
Jones Medical Research Building alongside the Emma Eccles Jones Foundation
([facilities history](arup-uofu-pathology-facilities.md)).

In the present tense, ARUP anchors the same "Genome Valley" cluster [Myriad](myriad-genetics.md)
names: university pathology, large commercial and nonprofit diagnostics labs, and genomics ventures
within a few miles on the Wasatch Front. ARUP is the piece whose distinguishing feature is university
ownership rather than public-company or venture-backed structure.

## Open Questions

- **Employee headcount is not established.** Neither the About page nor the department pages captured
  here publish a total workforce figure. Resolve against a Form 990, an annual report, or another
  primary record before asserting headcount.
- **Client hospital/laboratory count is not established.** ARUP describes serving hospital and
  health-system clients nationally but does not publish a client count on the captured pages.
- **Specimen volume is not established.** No annual specimen or requisition count appears in the
  captures.
- **Financial return to the University of Utah is described but not quantified.** The department
  states ARUP's directive is to "financially support the Department of Pathology"
  ([facilities history](arup-uofu-pathology-facilities.md)), but no transfer amount, operating
  margin, or revenue figure has been captured. A Form 990 or university financial disclosure would
  be the next step.
- **No identifier resolved yet.** No `ein`, `utah-entity`, or other registry key is recorded, so
  Class A records (990, state registration) have not been queried.
- **"Worldwide leader" and "leading national reference laboratory" are self-reported superlatives**
  from ARUP and the department; no independent market-share or ranking source has been captured.
- **The on-page fact sheet is undated.** Figures for test count, expert count, and on-site testing
  percentage may drift; date-attribute any future use against a fresh capture or filing.
