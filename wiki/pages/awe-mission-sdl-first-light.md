## Summary

A January 5, 2024 press release published on the official AWE mission site (awemission.org, run by USU's Space Dynamics Laboratory) announcing that the AWE instrument achieved "first light" on the ISS shortly after installation — confirming the SDL-built hardware was functioning as designed. This is the earliest of the four AWE source documents chronologically and the most detailed public statement of SDL's specific engineering role.

## Useful Claims

- First light confirmed the AMTM instrument's four infrared telescopes and subsystems were functioning after "a six-year effort to design, build, and test" them, per SDL mission operations manager Pedro Sevilla.
- States plainly that SDL "is responsible for AWE's total project management, systems engineering, safety and mission assurance, and on-orbit operations," while Ludger Scherliess leads the mission from USU's College of Science.
- AWE's Mission Operations Center is located at SDL facilities on USU's Innovation Campus in North Logan, UT — the specific Utah site where first-light images were received and reviewed.
- Describes SDL as headquartered on USU's Innovation Campus, a nonprofit and Department of Defense University Affiliated Research Center owned by USU, with more than 1,000 engineers, scientists, and staff and field offices in New Mexico, Virginia, Ohio, Alabama, Utah (Ogden), and elsewhere.
- Confirms AWE is a NASA Heliophysics Explorers Program "Mission of Opportunity" and names emeritus PI Michael Taylor alongside current PI Ludger Scherliess.

## Reliability Notes

Official mission-site press release from the instrument builder (SDL) with named engineers and specific facility/location detail not repeated as precisely elsewhere. High confidence for SDL's project-management and operations role and for the Innovation Campus location; treat "six-year effort" and headcount figures as SDL self-reported organizational claims.
