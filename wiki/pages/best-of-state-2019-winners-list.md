## Summary

The Best of State Foundation's directory of 2019 winners, printed in early 2020 and carrying the 2020
call for entries. It is the pre-rebuild counterpart to
[the 2025 program book](best-of-state-2025-winners-list.md), and it is the more valuable of the two
for one reason: it prints the **entry terms** — eligibility, self-nomination, the entry fee, the
deadline — which the current site distributes across several pages and states less plainly.

It also names all ten 2019 statue winners, which fills a real gap recorded on
[Best of State Awards](best-of-state-awards.md): before this capture the wiki could identify statue
winners for only three earlier years, because the older site generations listed medalists without
marking which of them took the division.

## Useful Claims

- **Eligibility is a Utah nexus and nothing more:** a nominee must live, work, or do business in
  Utah, and may be an individual, a business, or another organization.
- **Self-nomination is encouraged**, on the stated reasoning that nominees are best qualified to
  describe their own achievements. Nominator identity is withheld from judges.
- **Judging is on the nomination alone.** "All judging is based solely on information provided in the
  nomination" — so the award measures what an entrant submitted about itself, not what a panel went
  out and found.
- **A nominee may enter multiple categories**, which is the mechanism behind individuals and
  organizations accumulating several medals in one year.
- **Entry is paid.** "A nominal entry fee applies to each nomination." The amount is not printed here;
  the [nomination page](best-of-state-nomination-page.md) carries the current figures.
- **Ten general award divisions**, listed in full: Arts & Entertainment, Business Services, Community
  Development, Dining Establishments, Education, Hospitality Travel & Tourism, Merchandising &
  Consumer Services, Production & Manufacturing, Science & Technology, Sports & Recreation.
- **Judging criteria are stated as 50% overall excellence and achievement, 30% innovation and
  creativity, 20% making Utah a better place to live** — the same weighting
  [the 2025 program book](best-of-state-2025-winners-list.md) describes in prose.
- **The ten 2019 statue winners:** Hale Centre Theatre (Arts & Entertainment), Edge Steakhouse
  (Dining), Club V Volleyball (Sports & Recreation), SymbolArts (Production & Manufacturing), Odyssey
  House of Utah (Education), Blackrock Microsystems (Science & Technology), Webb (Business Services),
  Harmons Neighborhood Grocer (Merchandising & Consumer Services), Mayor Brent Taylor (Community
  Development), and Western River Expeditions / Moab Adventure Center (Hospitality, Travel & Tourism).
- **The program dates itself to 2002** here as well: the copyright line reads "© 2002-2019 Best of
  State".

## Reliability Notes

**Self-reported**, like every document this program publishes: it is authoritative about who the
program says won and about its own terms, and it is not independent evidence about any winner.

**Recovered from the Internet Archive.** The live path `bestofstate.org/pdf/2019_BOS_Winners_lowres.pdf`
returns HTTP 404 today, so `**URL:**` points at the Archive's raw (`id_`) copy of the 2022-04-06
snapshot — which is what was actually captured. The `id_` form matters: the ordinary snapshot URL
returns the Archive's own HTML wrapper, and a first attempt at this page captured that wrapper
instead of the document. The wrapper capture is a nine-line page about Archive Team and contains
none of the words quoted above.

**The document's own year labels are mixed, deliberately.** It is the *2019* winners directory and it
was printed for the *2020* entry season, so page footers read "© 2020 BEST OF STATE" and the call for
entries gives a March 15, 2020 deadline and a June 12, 2020 gala. Do not read either 2020 date as a
fact about the 2019 awards.

**The PDF's text layer has an encoding defect.** Registered-trademark and trade-mark glyphs extract as
mojibake (`ÂŽ`, `â˘`), which is why no quote above contains one. It is confined to those symbols; the
prose extracts cleanly.

**The `(1979-2018)` in the Community Development line is the winner's lifespan, not an award range** —
the 2019 statue in that division went to a mayor who had died the previous year.
