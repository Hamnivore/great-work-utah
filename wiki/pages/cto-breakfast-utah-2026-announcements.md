## Summary

Four monthly announcements posted to the Forge Utah Slack `#localevents` channel by **Derek Carter**
between March and June 2026, each announcing that month's CTO Breakfast and naming the next one.

Read as a series rather than individually, they are the only located record of the meeting **actually
recurring** — and of the one month in 2026 when the published "every second Friday" rule did not hold.

## Useful Claims

- The Utah CTO Breakfast ran on **March 13, April 10, May 8, and June 5, 2026**, each announced a few
  days ahead by the same organizer, at **IHOP in American Fork at 8am**.
- **June 2026 moved to the first Friday**, and the organizer flagged it as a departure — writing
  "1st Friday - CHANGE" — before the series returned to the second Friday on **July 10**.
- The invitation is consistent month to month: **"bring a friend and a topic to discuss"**, which
  describes an agenda set by attendees rather than by a program.
- The announcements link to [the chapter page](cto-breakfast-utah-official-page.md) as the canonical
  reference, tying the Slack series and the website to the same meeting.

## Verbatim

Each quote carries its speaker, channel, workspace, and date, because Slack message history is not
durable and the quote has to stand on its own once the original is gone. Slack link markup is reduced
to plain text; nothing else is altered.

## Reliability Notes

The Forge Utah Slack is an open community workspace and `#localevents` is a public announcement
channel, so these are public posts in every sense that matters for citation.

`testimony` rather than `official-page` because of **durability**, not secrecy: the permalink resolves
only for signed-in members, no script can fetch it signed out, the Internet Archive cannot crawl past
the login, and Slack retention will eventually delete the messages. Hence no `**Retrieved:**` and no
`**Archive:**` — their absence is the signal that this is a source nothing can re-fetch.

These are the organizer's own announcements, so they prove **scheduling and nothing else**. They do
not establish that anyone attended, how many, or what was discussed — and by design they could not:
the meeting runs under Chatham House Rules.

What makes the series worth capturing is the discrepancy it preserves. The website states the cadence
as a flat rule; these messages show the rule being suspended for a month and then restored, with the
organizer's own "CHANGE" marker. A reader working only from the website would have arrived at IHOP on
the wrong Friday in June 2026.

Two smaller cautions. The May message names June 5 as a *first* Friday and the June message calls
July 10 "Our Usual 2nd Friday", so the exception is bounded to one month by the organizer's own
account — but nothing here says **why** it moved. And the series located here begins in March 2026,
which is a limit of what was captured, not evidence about when the meeting started.
