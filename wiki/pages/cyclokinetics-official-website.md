## Summary

CycloKinetics' official website describes a Salt Lake City-based, venture-backed propellant technology company developing high-energy-density, drop-in fuels for aerospace and defense platforms, spun out of fifteen years of fuel R&D by CleanJoule founder Mukund Karanjikar. The site details a product line (CycloJP, CycloRP, CK-10), a Salt Lake City manufacturing facility operational since 2025, defense contract deliveries beginning Q2 2025, and a named leadership team and board.

## Useful Claims

- CycloKinetics develops "drop-in" propellants claimed to let turbine-powered platforms, UAS/C-UAS, long-range and attritable missiles, next-generation propulsion, and space launch systems fly farther, higher, faster, longer, and carry more payload without engine or infrastructure modification.
- The company reports "military testing across more than ten applications, including both ground and flight tests on currently fielded systems," with claimed measurable performance improvements — specifics and independent validation are not published on the site.
- Product line: CycloJP (JP-5/JP-8 drop-in replacement for turbines/UAS/CCA/C-UAS platforms), CycloRP (rocket-fuel application), and CK-10 (missile fuel).
- The company's Salt Lake City, UT industrial manufacturing facility became operational in 2025, with deliveries beginning under Department of War (formerly Department of Defense) contracts in Q2 2025; the process is described as feedstock-flexible to support both domestic and in-theater production.
- Leadership: Mukund Karanjikar, PhD (CEO and founder, also CleanJoule founder, 25+ years energy/chemicals, formerly Technology Holding and Chevron); Robert Price, PhD (CTO); Jeff King (CFO). Board includes Andrew Broderick (Indigo Partners), Ash Upadhyaya (Cleanhill Partners), and retired U.S. Air Force General Tod D. Wolters (former NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe).
- Self-describes as "venture-backed" without naming a specific round size, date, or full investor list on the public site.

## Reliability Notes

Official company website — strongest source for product naming, team, and self-described manufacturing timeline, but self-reported for all performance and testing claims ("measurable improvements," "military testing across more than ten applications"). No performance data, contract values, or named defense customers are published. The site's News section was non-functional (placeholder Lorem Ipsum text) at time of access. Corroborate company-founding and launch-date claims against the May 2026 GlobeNewswire launch release and Flying magazine's profile before treating them as settled; the [Flying](https://www.flyingmag.com/cyclokinetics-fuel-military-farther-faster/) profile puts CleanJoule's founding at 2009, earlier than CleanJoule's own "around 2020" page estimate, and cites propellant energy-density gains "up to 32 percent" — a figure not on CycloKinetics' own site.
