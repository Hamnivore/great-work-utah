## Summary

Final audit report **ED-OIG/A05M0009**, September 2017, titled "Western Governors University Was Not
Eligible to Participate in the Title IV Programs." The Office of Inspector General examined whether
WGU's competency-based courses met the federal definition of **distance education**, which requires
"regular and substantive interaction between students and instructors." The OIG concluded they did
not, reclassified them as **correspondence courses**, and recommended the Department recover
**$712,670,616** in Title IV funds.

This is the first primary-tier source on the Western Governors page and the only captured document
that scrutinizes the mechanism the rest of that page describes. Every other WGU source in this wiki
is the university's own marketing copy.

**Read the recommendation as a recommendation.** The OIG audits and advises; Federal Student Aid
decides. This capture establishes what the OIG found and asked for. It does **not** establish what
the Department ultimately did, and the wiki has not captured that disposition.

## Useful Claims

- Report number **ED-OIG/A05M0009**, dated **September 2017**, 93 pages.
- The OIG concluded WGU "**became ineligible to participate in the Title IV programs as of June 30,
  2014**."
- Recommended recovery of **$712,670,616** received from **July 1, 2014 through June 30, 2016**, plus
  any funds received after that date.
- The finding rests on the **faculty model**: the OIG concluded WGU's course design did not ensure
  regular and substantive interaction between students and instructors, and that interaction was
  primarily **student-initiated** and available **on an as-needed basis**.
- The OIG examined **69 courses** and concluded none of them met the distance-education requirement.
- WGU's own response is carried **inside the report** as an appendix, so the university's rebuttal is
  part of this same primary document rather than a separate source.
- The OIG's own text acknowledges the terms at issue are undefined: neither "regular" nor
  "substantive" is defined in the Higher Education Act or its regulations — which is the crux of the
  dispute rather than a footnote to it.

## Reliability Notes

Primary tier at a mandated host: a federal Inspector General's final audit report, published by the
agency, retrieved as a PDF and extracted to text by `scripts/lib/pdf-text.mjs`. The capture is 93
pages of extracted text; quotes here are literal substrings of it.

**What this document is, and what it is not.** An OIG audit is an internal watchdog's finding and
recommendation. It is authoritative evidence of *what the OIG concluded*, and it is not a
determination of law, a court ruling, or a final agency action. Federal Student Aid holds the
decision authority over whether to seek recovery. Secondary reporting indicates the Department
declined to pursue the recommended repayment, citing the ambiguity of the regulations during the
audit period; **that disposition is not captured here and is therefore not asserted.** It is the
obvious next capture, and until it exists this page should not be cited as though the finding stood.

The dispute is also of general significance beyond WGU: "regular and substantive interaction" is the
line separating distance education from correspondence education for Title IV purposes, and the
Department issued further guidance on it in the years after this audit. A page using this source to
weigh competency-based education should treat the audit as one side of a live regulatory argument,
not as a settled verdict on the model.
