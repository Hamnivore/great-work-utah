## Summary

Evolution Bio is a Salt Lake City biotechnology company, founded circa 2022, built around directed protein evolution for drug discovery — especially G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs). GPCRs are the highest-value target class in pharmacology — roughly a third of approved drugs act on them — and among the hardest to engineer.

## Impact

GPCRs are the highest-value target class in pharmacology — roughly a third of approved drugs act on them — and among the hardest to engineer, because variants selected in bacteria or yeast frequently behave differently in mammalian pharmacology. VEGAS attacks that mismatch directly by performing selection in mammalian cells, with reported 24-hour selection cycles and week-scale campaigns across transcription factors, GPCRs, and nanobodies. A commercial platform that reliably delivers mammalian-evolved GPCR variants would compress discovery cycles across an enormous target space, and the depth of that displacement is what puts the page in the middle of the ladder rather than the bottom.

Two discounts keep it there. First, the ceiling belongs to the field, and a page gets the share it would actually cause: mammalian directed evolution has several credible pursuers — yeast-based GPCR evolution and vaccinia-based mammalian systems among them — so "if this works, drug discovery changes" is not the same claim as "if *we* are the ones who make it work." Second, VEGAS was published in *Cell* in 2019 and its components are available on Addgene, which means the method is not the moat; the company's counterfactual contribution is industrializing something an adequately funded lab could otherwise run itself. Independent reviews have also questioned continuous Sindbis-based evolution on packaging competition, cheater emergence, and alphavirus biosafety — real limits on claiming production readiness.

**Bet:** that selection pressure applied in mammalian cells produces protein variants that survive the translation to human pharmacology often enough to be worth buying, and that a services-and-licensing platform is the right business shape for capturing that value rather than a single-asset drug company.

## What They Are Building

Evolution Bio's model, inferred from investor and incubator descriptions rather than from company documents, is platform infrastructure: apply directed evolution to partners' therapeutic candidates across target classes, through licensing or contract engineering, rather than developing a proprietary drug pipeline. Altitude Lab describes it as "a biotechnology company using its directed evolution technology to optimize drug candidates across a broad range of therapeutic targets," and third-party profiles add laboratory automation, iterative selection, and data-driven screening as the operating method.

The English Lab continues active VEGAS expansion — new selection paradigms, nanobody libraries for GPCR control, GPCR pharmacogenomics — which plausibly feeds the company's technology base. Where the corporate IP boundary sits relative to ongoing academic work is not documented in any source cited here.

## Utah Context

Evolution Bio is a University of Utah biochemistry spinout backed by Utah's Nucleus Fund, the university itself, and Altitude Lab, the Recursion-affiliated incubator in University of Utah Research Park. It connects the state's academic GPCR pharmacology depth to the Wasatch Front's platform-biotech cluster, and it is one of the smaller entries in an Altitude Lab portfolio — 52 companies across residents and alumni — that is the main engine turning University of Utah life-science research into companies.
