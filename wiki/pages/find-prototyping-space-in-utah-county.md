# Find Prototyping and Project Space in Utah County

**Type:** guide
**Status:** Useful
**Focus:** Utah County, makerspace, prototype space, hardware workspace, coworking, project space, builder community
**Updated:** 2026-08-11

Someone looking for a makerspace in Utah County has several places to make, test, or discuss a physical project, but they solve different problems. The right first question is not “which makerspace is best?” It is “what must happen in the space this week?”

## Choose By The Work

### Make one small test with public equipment

Start with [Orem Library Makerspace](orem-library-makerspace.md) · https://greatutah.work/pages/orem-library-makerspace.md. It is the lowest-commitment route for occasional 3D printing, recording, digitization, and small creative-tool use. Check the live equipment rules before planning around a machine; library access is not the same as an unrestricted workshop.

### Build a startup prototype and also need mentors

[iHub](ihub.md) · https://greatutah.work/pages/ihub.md combines a maker space with coworking, mentors, a commercial kitchen, and warehouse capacity. Its public material is strongest for founders moving through a program, not for someone seeking anonymous hourly shop rental. Ask explicitly whether the relevant equipment or workspace is available to nonmembers and what approval or fee applies.

### Need sustained hard-tech R&D space

[Make Utah](make-utah.md) · https://greatutah.work/pages/make-utah.md is the stronger lead for a hardware or manufacturing venture that needs ongoing prototyping infrastructure and a peer community comfortable with long development cycles. Public pricing and shop-certification rules are incomplete, so contact the organization with the exact process, footprint, power, ventilation, storage, and access requirements.

### Need student teams or university-linked project work

[UVU Innovation Academy](utah-valley-university-innovation-center-academy.md) · https://greatutah.work/pages/utah-valley-university-innovation-center-academy.md routes UVU students, faculty, and external partners into applied projects. It is a partnership channel, not a public machine shop. A community builder should ask whether the need fits a student project, internship, capstone, or mentorship program before treating it as workspace.

### Need feedback and other builders, not equipment

[1 Million Cups Orem](1-million-cups-orem.md) · https://greatutah.work/pages/1-million-cups-orem.md is a recurring founder-feedback route. Use it to pressure-test the project and find local collaborators; do not confuse an entrepreneurship gathering with a place that can safely host fabrication.

## Ask Before Visiting

Send the facility a short, concrete description:

1. What material or object are you bringing?
2. Which operation must happen—printing, cutting, soldering, assembly, recording, testing, storage, or public demonstration?
3. What footprint, power, ventilation, noise control, network access, and safety conditions does it require?
4. Do you need one appointment, recurring access, or a dedicated area?
5. Can the work be public, or does it involve confidential IP or controlled data?

A “yes, we have tools” answer is not enough. Confirm member eligibility, required training, reservation lead time, materials rules, insurance or waiver requirements, storage, and total cost before moving equipment.
