## Summary

**Hope Squad** is a school-based **peer suicide-prevention** program founded by **Dr. Gregory A.
Hudnall** after youth suicides in **Provo City School District**
([mission history](hope-squad-mission-history.md)). Peer-nominated students are trained to recognize
warning signs and refer classmates to trusted adults — explicitly **not** to act as counselors
([official site](hope-squad-official-website.md)).

The public brand operates from **5455 N River Run Dr., Provo** under the legal entity **Hope 4
Utah** (EIN **90-0969731**, tax-exempt since **July 2014**, NTEE crisis-intervention services)
([Form 990 record](hope-4-utah-form-990-record.md);
[mission history](hope-squad-mission-history.md)). The organization reports Hope Squads in
**thousands of schools across the United States and Canada**
([mission history](hope-squad-mission-history.md)) — a **self-reported, undated adoption claim**.

## Impact

The Provo origin story is specific. Hudnall, then a principal, was called to identify a student who
died by suicide in **1997**; after a later loss where peers withheld warning signs from adults, he
piloted the first squad at **Timpview High** with **forty** nominated students
([mission history](hope-squad-mission-history.md)). Hope Squad claims **zero suicides in Provo City
School District for nine years after implementation** — an outcome statement on the organization's
own site, **not independently verified** in this shard.

National scale is asserted, not audited: **"thousands of schools"** in the **U.S. and Canada**
([mission history](hope-squad-mission-history.md)), plus homepage survey claims (**98%** of
administrators report positive climate; **95%** of students know how to get peers help)
([official site](hope-squad-official-website.md)). None of these figures carry a publication date or
external evaluator on the captured pages.

The evidence base is **real but narrower than the footprint**. Hope Squad's research page lists
**peer-reviewed papers** with Utah-affiliated co-authors — including *Children & Schools* (2019) on
suicide concern reporting and *Journal of Human Behavior in the Social Environment* (2023) on
gatekeeper training outcomes ([research page](hope-squad-research-page.md)). A formal effectiveness
study is listed **under review**, not published. Internal **2023–2024 referral-form** data claiming
**95%+** of referrals from non-squad members is organization-reported
([research page](hope-squad-research-page.md)). **The gap between adoption rhetoric and published
evaluation is the interesting finding**: peer-reviewed work exists, but it does not yet substantiate
"thousands of schools" or district-wide suicide reductions at national scale.

FY ending **Dec. 2025**: revenue **$357,456** (mostly **program services**), expenses **$427,601**
([Form 990 record](hope-4-utah-form-990-record.md)) — a small Utah nonprofit whose earned income
suggests training/licensing fees, not a volunteer-only club.

**Gregory Hudnall** is the organization's founder and director.

## Utah Context

Founded in **Provo** and still headquartered there. The Provo City School District pilot remains the
documented proof case in the organization's own narrative. Utah County is the corpus anchor; national
expansion is the breadth story.

## What They Need Now

Hope Squad sells **implementation support** to schools and districts ([official site](hope-squad-official-website.md)):
partnerships with foundations and mental health nonprofits, advisor training, and curriculum. Captured
pages imply ongoing needs in **program delivery**, **school outreach**, **research/evaluation**, and
**operations** — inferred from "Start a Hope Squad" and partner pathways, not from a careers board.
