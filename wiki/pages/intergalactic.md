## Summary

Intergalactic is a St. George aerospace company that designs and builds compact thermal-management systems, components, controls, and software for civil aircraft, defense platforms, spacecraft, and demanding land and sea applications. Its current catalog includes active and passive cooling systems, heat exchangers, pumps, compressors, controllers, fans, heaters, accumulators, valves, and power-distribution equipment.

This is a particularly actionable Utah frontier employer because the work crosses mechanical and electrical engineering, embedded controls, systems integration, qualification, cleanroom assembly, and aerospace manufacturing rather than sitting in only one discipline.

## Impact

Heat is a hard constraint on dense electronics, high-energy systems, electric aircraft, satellites, and crewed vehicles. Lighter and more capable thermal systems can return mass, volume, and electrical power to the mission payload while allowing higher-performance electronics to operate reliably. Intergalactic's potential impact is therefore enabling: it rises with the number and consequence of aircraft and spacecraft programs whose capabilities would otherwise be limited by heat rejection.

The downside bounds are also real. The company serves defense as well as civil and space customers, so better thermal management can increase the capability of military platforms. Public sources do not expose enough program detail to estimate that balance or Intergalactic's counterfactual contribution.

**Bet:** Aerospace thermal management is sufficiently stagnant and sufficiently mission-limiting that a specialist supplier can unlock meaningful vehicle-level gains rather than merely replace incumbent components.

## Utah Context

Intergalactic says it is headquartered at 3172 E. Deseret Drive South in St. George. Its solutions page describes a current facility with 10,000 square feet of cleanroom space and a planned 70,000-square-foot facility on Tech Ridge. The company grew from Airborne Environmental Control Systems after St. George-based RAM Company acquired a controlling interest and relocated the operation from Washington to Utah in 2019.

That history makes Intergalactic part of a specific southern Utah aerospace lineage rather than an isolated outpost. [RAM Aviation, Space & Defense](ram-aviation-space-defense.md) supplies precision propulsion and fluid-control components nearby; Intergalactic concentrates on integrated thermal systems and environmental control.
