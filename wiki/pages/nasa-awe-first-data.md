## Summary

NASA's article announces the first public scientific-data release from the Atmospheric Waves Experiment after its 3,000th orbit aboard the International Space Station. It describes the released imagery and derived products, the instrument, the processing work, and Utah State University's mission leadership.

## Useful Claims

- The first 3,000 orbits of AWE data were publicly released in March 2025.
- The release contained more than five million images from four cameras, plus derived temperature and airglow-intensity swaths.
- AWE's Advanced Mesospheric Temperature Mapper consists of four identical telescopes mounted outside the International Space Station.
- USU researchers and students developed software to align the telescopes' imagery and address cloud, ground, moonlight, city-light, solar-panel, and stray-light interference.
- NASA states that USU leads AWE, SDL built the instrument and provides mission operations, and NASA Goddard's Explorers Program Office manages the mission.

## Reliability Notes

This is an official NASA account and strong evidence for the release date, scale and composition of the dataset, instrument design, and named institutional roles. Descriptions of the dataset as unprecedented and predictions of future scientific benefit are mission-team assessments, not substitutes for peer-reviewed results.
