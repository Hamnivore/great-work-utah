## Summary


Neumont is a small private for-profit college in downtown Salt Lake City devoted almost entirely to computer science ([IPEDS](neumont-ipeds-directory-record.md)). Its narrowness is the point: Utah has few degree-granting schools organized around building software for real companies rather than placing a CS major inside a broad curriculum.

## Impact

Neumont's significance in Utah is **structural rather than headline**. It is not large and it is not
famous, but it occupies a slot almost nothing else in the state fills: a degree-granting institution
whose entire program is software, organized around building things for real companies rather than
around a general curriculum with a CS major inside it.

The measurable evidence of that focus is in the federal record and is unusually stark. Of the school's
24 faculty, **zero** are classified in research and public service — all are instructional. This is a
teaching institution with no research arm, which is exactly what the Carnegie special-focus
classification implies and is the opposite of how the state's other computer science capacity is
organized at the University of Utah, BYU, or Utah State.

Its second role is as **community infrastructure**. The campus is a venue the local technology
community actually uses — Utah Kids Code Camp has been hosted there
([Kids Code Camp 2026 announcements](utah-kids-code-camp-2026-announcements.md)) — which is a small
thing that recurs, and the kind of contribution a downtown campus can make that a suburban one cannot.

What this page cannot assess is whether the model works. **No graduation rate, employment outcome,
loan repayment rate, or student debt figure has been pulled**, and for a for-profit institution those
are the numbers that matter most.

## Utah Context

Neumont sits at **143 South Main Street**, in the core of downtown Salt Lake City rather than on a
campus at the edge of a suburb — with student housing attached, which is unusual for an institution of
its size and makes it a genuinely residential downtown presence.

It fills a specific gap in the state's education-to-employment pipeline. Utah's computer science
capacity is concentrated in large public universities and, more recently, in bootcamps and
apprenticeship programs; Neumont is neither. It is accredited and degree-granting like the former,
narrow and employer-facing like the latter, and it has been running that combination since 2003 — long
before the bootcamp wave.

Its **enterprise projects and enterprise partnerships** are the mechanism that ties it to Utah
employers, putting students on outside companies' problems as coursework. That is the part of the
model most relevant to this wiki's charter, and also the part with the least independent evidence
behind it.

## What They Need Now

Nothing located states Neumont's own hiring or institutional needs, and this section should not invent
them. What the record does show is the shape of the institution's dependencies:

- **Employer partners for enterprise projects.** The model requires a steady supply of companies
  willing to put real work in front of students. That is the school's structural need, and the most
  direct way for a Utah company to engage with it.
- **Instructional faculty.** With 13 full-time faculty covering a 27-to-1 student ratio, teaching
  capacity is the binding constraint on growth, and there is no research track to subsidize it.

Anyone considering Neumont as a student should ask for outcome data directly — completion rates,
placement rates, and median debt — because those are the figures this page cannot supply and the ones
a for-profit college should be asked for first.

## Who Could Help

Utah employers willing to sponsor enterprise projects or hire from a small local pipeline; people who
can supply or interpret Title IV outcome data for prospective students; and anyone with primary
records on the school's accreditation history and its repeated name changes.

## Open Questions

- **What are the outcomes?** Graduation rate, employment rate, median earnings, and median debt are all
  unpulled. For a Title IV-participating for-profit college these are the load-bearing figures, and
  College Scorecard publishes them.
- **Which name is current, and why does it drift?** The federal record files it as Neumont College of
  Computer Science; the website brands Neumont University in the same breath as it prints the other
  name in the footer. No record of a formal name change was located.
- **Is the NWCCU accreditation claim confirmed by NWCCU?** Only the school's own assertion has been
  captured. The accreditor's directory would settle it and has not been cited.
- **Who owns it?** It is private for-profit; no ownership record, parent company, or investor was
  located.
- **What is the enterprise project program's actual scale?** Which companies participate, how many
  students, and what the projects produce are all unestablished.
- **How has enrollment moved?** 476 is a single undated snapshot from one reporting cycle. A small
  institution's trend line matters more than its level, and the trend was not pulled.
