## Summary

Four monthly meeting announcements posted to the Forge Utah Slack `#localevents` channel by Phil
Gilmore between March and July 2026. Together they are the only located evidence that NUNUG is
*currently operating*, because [its own website](nunug-official-website.md) has a news feed that stops
in 2020 and a "next meeting" slot reading TBA.

They also date a restart. The March 2026 announcement calls that meeting a "comeback meeting", which
is the simplest explanation for a live group behind a dormant-looking site.

## Useful Claims

- NUNUG resumed meeting in **March 2026** after a gap, and the March meeting was announced as a
  comeback meeting.
- Meetings ran monthly and consecutively from March through July 2026, on the second Thursday, at
  6:30 PM, hybrid — in person at Mindfire's Layton office and online via Microsoft Teams.
- Named sessions and presenters: **2026-03-12** "Comeback meeting — Catch up with .NET!" (George Ray);
  **2026-04-09** "High Performance C#" (Nate Zaugg); **2026-06-11** "Test Driven Development with AI"
  (George Ray); **2026-07-09** "The Good, The Bad, and The Hallucinated: AI Coding in .NET" (Ken
  Bailey), held with the group's annual BBQ.
- Programming has turned toward AI-assisted development: three of the four announced topics concern
  AI in a .NET context.
- Food policy varies by meeting and is stated in each announcement ("come fed" for regular meetings,
  "come hungry" for the BBQ).

## Reliability Notes

The Forge Utah Slack is an **open community workspace**, joinable by anyone from the foundation's
homepage, and `#localevents` is a public announcement channel. Material posted there is public in the
sense that matters for citation — comparable to a public forum post — and these are event
announcements written to be spread as widely as possible, not private conversation.

What still makes this `testimony` rather than `official-page` is durability, not privacy. The
permalink resolves only once you are signed in to the workspace, so no script can fetch it and the
Internet Archive cannot snapshot it; Slack retention will eventually remove the message regardless of
anyone's intent. That is why this page carries no `**Retrieved:**`, no `**Archive:**`, and no
`**Raw:**` — their absence is the honest signal — and why the quotes above are reproduced at length.
The governing rule is in `meta/attributes.md` under "Community-channel testimony"; the capture
question it raises is worked through in `research/raw-data/class-c-private-ephemeral-sources.md`.

On tier: these are the group's own organizer describing his own group, so on subject matter they sit
level with the group's website. Their value is not authority but **recency and dating**. They are
consecutive, each is timestamped, and they are the only thing distinguishing an active group from an
abandoned one.

The hard limit: an announcement is a plan. This source establishes that meetings were *scheduled*,
with a named presenter and topic, on a stated date. It is not evidence that any meeting took place or
that anyone attended, and no page should cite it for attendance.
