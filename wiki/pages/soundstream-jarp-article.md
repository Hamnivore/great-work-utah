## Summary

Simon Barber's peer-reviewed case study in the *Journal on the Art of Record Production* (November 2012) on Soundstream's introduction of commercial digital recording in the United States, 1975–1983. It draws on interviews with Soundstream staff and recording-industry participants plus published technical literature; it is the load-bearing scholarly account for founding chronology, system design, market adoption, and competition with Denon, 3M, Sony, and others.

## Useful Claims

- Soundstream was incorporated in 1975 in Salt Lake City, Utah, and was the first commercial digital audio recording company in the United States.
- Dr. Thomas G. Stockham Jr. founded Soundstream at the University of Utah in May 1975.
- Soundstream built professional digital recorders and computer-based digital editing used in commercial recording sessions during the 1970s and early 1980s, before the compact disc reached consumers.
- Stockham's editing system is described as a direct precursor of the modern digital audio workstation.
- Soundstream ceased operating in 1983 amid competition from larger companies and the rise of the CD standard.
- The article places Soundstream beside parallel systems from Denon, 3M, Sony, and others and cautions against reading "first" claims without that context.

## Reliability Notes

Peer-reviewed recording-history scholarship, stronger than general encyclopedic summaries for technical chronology and session detail. Still not a primary record for incorporation dates (Utah corporate registration would settle May 1975) or for patent-level engineering claims (see [U.S. Patent 4,202,018 — Stockham / Soundstream, Error Recognition and Correction](soundstream-error-correction-patent-us4202018.md)).
