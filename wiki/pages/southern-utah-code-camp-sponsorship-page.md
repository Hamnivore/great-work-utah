## Summary

The Southern Utah Code Camp sponsorship pitch. Its value is unusual for an event page: it publishes
**an itemized budget** for the coming edition, stating line by line what a 24-hour community hackathon
in St. George costs to run.

It also sets out four cumulative sponsorship tiers and — worth reading before registering as a
participant — the benefits sponsors receive, which include **the participant contact list after the
event**.

## Useful Claims

- The **estimated 2026 budget is $13,000–$14,300**, itemized across four lines: **meals and snacks**
  (the largest, at $6,000–$6,500), **participant shirts**, **prizes and awards**, and **tables and
  chairs**.
- The event is **funded entirely** by businesses, organizations, and community members; the organizers
  state every sponsored dollar is spent directly on the participant experience.
- Sponsorship runs in **four cumulative tiers** — Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Platinum — with each tier
  including everything from the tiers below it. The range runs from **$ 250** at Bronze to
  **$ 2500** at Platinum.
- Benefits listed across the tiers include **logo on the website**, **logo on the main screen during
  the event**, **access to all participants during the conference**, **the ability to distribute
  materials**, **an introduction during the Friday lunch session**, **a company banner**, **logo on
  the printed flyer**, and **the participant contact list after the event**.
- **Three exclusive sponsorships** are offered for 2026, attached to signature parts of the event —
  one of them the participant shirt, carrying the sponsor's logo during the event and afterward.

## Reliability Notes

Self-reported tier, and specifically a **fundraising** document. That governs how every number on it
should be read.

The budget is the reason to cite this page, and it carries the sharpest caveat: it is labelled an
**estimate for 2026**, published to persuade sponsors. It states what the organizers say the event
will cost, and it must never be cited as what the event actually cost or raised. No accounting, audit,
or prior-year actuals appear anywhere on the site.

**A limitation of the capture, not the page:** the tier benefits are rendered with per-tier markers
that do not survive text extraction, so the captured text repeats the **same full benefit list under
all four tiers**. The individual benefits and the four price points are reliable; **which benefits
attach to which tier is not recoverable from this capture** and should not be asserted. The one firm
structural claim is the page's own — that tiers are cumulative.

Two things worth drawing out for readers who are not sponsors:

- **The participant contact list is a sponsor benefit.** Anyone registering for this event should
  expect their contact details to be shared with sponsoring companies afterward. The sponsorship page
  says so plainly; whether the registration flow says so was not checked.
- **The budget is entirely participant-facing.** All four line items — food, shirts, prizes,
  furniture — go to running the event. No line covers organizer compensation, venue rental, or
  overhead, which is consistent with a volunteer-run event in donated space, though the page does not
  say so.
