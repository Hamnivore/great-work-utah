## Summary


Southern Utah Code Camp is a 24-hour Saint George app-building competition ([official site](southern-utah-code-camp-official-website.md)). Experience-based divisions stop professionals from carrying novice teams, making it unusually credible for young local builders while keeping its impact event-scale.

## Who It's For

Deliberately stratified, which is the point. The divisions run from **Novice** — some coding
experience, typically high-school or first-year college students — through **Intermediate**, for
people with real skill who have never been paid for it, to **Industry**, for teams containing at least
one working professional. "Professional" is defined broadly enough to catch an unpaid intern of more
than two years.

Placement is by **ceiling, not average**: a team of one professional and three novices competes in the
Industry division. That rule is what keeps the lower divisions worth entering, and it is the thing to
understand before assembling a team.

A fourth track, **Day Camp**, is Novice-level but **does not run overnight** — it finishes at 3:00 PM
on Friday. That is the quiet accessibility feature in the design: it makes the event reachable for
high-school-aged participants and anyone who cannot stay out all night, without carving out a separate
event for them.

The organizers frame the divisions by motive as well as skill — Industry teams testing company ideas
or side projects "or simply to just claim bragging rights", Intermediate teams building something to
show potential employers.

**Project categories run at right angles to all of this.** Teams self-select a category — App, Game,
and others — the top team in each wins, and categories are explicitly *not* limited by division. A
novice team's app competes against an industry team's app. So the event is a protected ladder and an
open contest simultaneously, depending on which prize you are chasing.

## What It Provides

Twenty-four hours, a venue, meals, and a judged competition with prizes — plus the Pre-Flight session
the night before, which the organizers strongly encourage attending because rules are finalized and
teams are formed there rather than at the starting gun.

The sponsor-funded budget for 2026 is published in full: an estimated **$13,000–$14,300**, across four
lines — meals and snacks, participant shirts, prizes and awards, and tables and chairs. Every line is
participant-facing; nothing covers organizer pay or overhead. For a sponsor, four cumulative tiers run
**$250, $500, $1000, and $2500**, with benefits from logo placement up through access to participants
and the post-event contact list, plus three exclusive sponsorships attached to signature parts of the
event.

That last benefit is worth naming plainly: **at some tiers, sponsors receive the participant contact
list after the event.** Anyone registering should expect that.

## How To Use It

Register through `southernutahcodecamp.com`, which carries the current year's dates, rules, venue, and
under-18 waiver. Then:

- **Attend the Pre-Flight** the evening before. Rules change year to year and teams finalize there.
- **Pick your division honestly** — placement follows your most experienced member, and the site
  defines "professional" broadly enough to include anyone paid for development work or interning more
  than two years.
- **Arrive without a team if you need to.** Teams of one are permitted, and the Pre-Flight exists
  partly to pair people up.
- **Sponsors and volunteers** have their own paths on the site; the event runs on both.

Because it recurs, check the site for the current edition rather than relying on the dates here.

## Cost / Eligibility

No ticket price is stated on the captured pages, and the budget shows the event covering meals,
shirts, and venue from sponsorship. Participants under 18 must submit a waiver. Verify the current
year's terms on the site — nothing located confirms whether registration is free.

## Best Fits

A student or self-taught developer in Washington County who wants a deadline, a team, and a judged
result without traveling to Salt Lake or Provo — and, at the other end, a working professional in
Southern Utah who wants to test an idea overnight among peers. The divisional structure means both can
attend the same event without one making the other pointless.

For a company in the St. George area, the sponsorship tiers are a cheap way to be visible to the
region's technical talent pool at a moment when it is all in one room.

## Open Questions

- **Is registration free for participants?** The budget implies sponsor-funded attendance but no page
  captured states a participant price either way.
- **What is Tech Threads, INC?** The homepage footer states the event is "organized and run by Tech
  Threads, INC", but **no corporate registration has been located** for it. It is absent from the IRS
  Utah exempt-organization data, consistent with a for-profit entity or one registered under another
  name — unresolved, and worth resolving, since it is the body receiving the sponsorship money.
- **How long has it run, and how many attend?** The site says the format has run "for years" without
  naming a first edition, and **no attendance figure appears anywhere** for any year.
- **Did the November 2026 edition take place?** This page was written in August 2026, three months
  ahead. Nothing here establishes the outcome, the sponsors, or the winners.
- **What is the fifth division?** The About page announces five and gives headings for four — Industry,
  Intermediate, Novice, and Day Camp. Its own worked example additionally names a **"Collegiate"**
  level that is never defined, which is almost certainly the fifth but is not stated as such.
- **Are participants told at registration** that sponsors receive the contact list afterward? The
  sponsorship page says so plainly; the registration flow was not checked.
- **What happens to the participant contact list** that sponsors receive, and what are entrants told
  about it at registration?
