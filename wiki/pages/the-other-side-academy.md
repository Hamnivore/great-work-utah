## Summary

The Other Side Academy runs a long-term residential therapeutic community, often as an alternative to incarceration ([how it works](the-other-side-academy-how-it-works.md)). Its related [Other Side Village](the-other-side-village-official-website.md) tests whether peer accountability can support permanent exits from chronic homelessness.

## Impact

The depth argument is about people the criminal-justice and shelter systems have largely written
off: the academy explicitly targets those with long histories of addiction and crime, uses a
**peer-led "classic therapeutic community"** model rather than clinical staff, and asks for a
**30-month** stay — far longer than typical treatment or jail-diversion programs
([official site](the-other-side-academy-official-website.md),
[how it works](the-other-side-academy-how-it-works.md)). If the model works as advertised, the
counterfactual is repeated incarceration, homelessness, and overdose cycles at public expense. The
Delancey Street lineage is widely reported and is how the organization is usually described, but it
rests here on the organization's uncaptured history page, so this wiki names it as an attribution
rather than a fact.

Breadth is split across geographies and projects. Site navigation lists social enterprises in **Salt
Lake City** (Moving & Storage, Thrift Boutique, Builders) and **Denver** (Designer Storage, Moving
& Storage, Furniture Boutique) ([official site](the-other-side-academy-official-website.md)), plus
the separate **Other Side Village** housing development reporting **60 completed housing units
(Phase I)** in Salt Lake City
([village site](the-other-side-village-official-website.md)). Endorsements from Utah political
figures appear on the Village site; those establish visibility, not program efficacy.

Permanence looks solid, and for the first time it can be checked against a filing rather than a
claim. The academy describes itself as **self-sufficient**, with training-school revenues covering
**most** — not all — program expenses
([how it works](the-other-side-academy-how-it-works.md)). The IRS record puts numbers on it: program
service revenue grew substantially across the filing history, while contributions remained the larger
source of revenue ([Form 990 record](the-other-side-academy-form-990-record.md)). The filing record
supports the direction of that comparison, but exact annual values should be re-derived from the
underlying return before repeating them.

What no captured source establishes is whether the model works. **No independent evaluation of The
Other Side Academy's outcomes has been located**; the organization publishes statistics on a
`/results` page not captured here. This page also declines to characterize the research literature on
residential therapeutic communities — the usual criticisms concern selection effects, peer coercion,
and self-reported outcome numerators, but the wiki has captured no review to cite, and an uncited
skeptical claim is no better sourced than an uncited promotional one.

**Bet:** that a Delancey Street–style community — **no tuition, no therapists, heavy peer
accountability, and businesses staffed by students** — can habilitate people who have never seen an
orderly life, where shorter clinical programs fail. The Academy states it takes **no government
money**; that claim is scoped to the Academy, no captured source addresses the Village's funding, and
the Village site does advertise public cost savings while soliciting corporate support. The bet is
contrarian in Utah social services and puts enormous weight on peer culture and work as therapy.

The honest counterweight: excluding people who need clinical dual-diagnosis care, restricting campus
departure, and relying on students to run moving and thrift businesses creates real failure modes
and ethical scrutiny. Outcome figures, if cited at all, must be treated as **self-reported
organizational claims** until independently verified.

## Utah Context

Headquartered in **Salt Lake City** at **661 E 100 S** — the historic Armstrong Mansion — which the
organization's own pages state and which the IRS registry record independently confirms as its
address of record ([Form 990 record](the-other-side-academy-form-990-record.md)).

The academy's origin and its political backing are **not established from any captured source**. The
organization's History page, which is where the Utah County jail-diversion account and the John
Curtis connection appear, has not been captured, and this page therefore asserts neither. Current
endorsements from SLC Mayor Erin Mendenhall and Rep. John Curtis do appear on the captured Village
site.

The Village adds a visible Utah experiment in **permanent supportive housing** for chronic
homelessness — a policy area where Salt Lake City has struggled for years — though the captured
Village text describes **housing units**, not "tiny homes" specifically.

## What They Need Now

The organization's public-facing needs split three ways:

**Students who want change.** Admission is voluntary; the site emphasizes coming in person to
request an interview. The academy does not accept sex offenders, arsonists, or dual-diagnosis
applicants needing specialized counseling
([official site](the-other-side-academy-official-website.md)).

**Customers and contract work for social enterprises.** Moving, storage, thrift, furniture, and
construction businesses employ students and fund operations — commercial volume directly supports
the model ([official site](the-other-side-academy-official-website.md),
[how it works](the-other-side-academy-how-it-works.md)).

**Donors, volunteers, and judicial partners.** Donation links and "How To Help" navigation are
prominent; many students arrive through court-directed alternatives to incarceration
([how it works](the-other-side-academy-how-it-works.md)). The Village separately solicits
volunteers, sponsors, and tour visitors
([village site](the-other-side-village-official-website.md)).

Operational hiring (program staff, directors, HR) is visible on team pages but no general careers
landing page was found.

## Open Questions

- **Academy outcome statistics are not established here.** The organization publishes a `/results`
  page that has not been captured, so this wiki does not know which metrics it reports or as of when.
  Capturing it is the obvious next step; even then the figures would be attributed self-reports, not
  wiki facts, until an independent evaluation exists.
- **No independent or peer-reviewed evaluation located** for The Other Side Academy or The Other Side
  Village. The how-it-works page references a research white paper; neither the white paper nor
  third-party audits were captured.
- **Denver campus is partially documented.** Site navigation lists Denver enterprises and a Denver
  team page, but the captured sources do not quote a Denver street address or the word "campus." The
  Denver street address appears on the How To Apply page, which has not been captured, so this page
  does not state it.
- **Legal and governance relationship between the Academy and the Village** is unclear from captured
  sources — shared branding and endorsements, but no corporate structure verified.
- **Resolved: registry identity.** EIN **47-4495796**, 501(c)(3) confirmed by IRS subsection code
  and a December 2015 ruling date, address 661 E 100 S
  ([Form 990 record](the-other-side-academy-form-990-record.md)). Still open: **whether the Academy
  receives government money**, which it says it does not. Form 990 Part VIII separates government
  grants from other contributions and would settle it; the filing PDFs are linked from the
  organization's Financial Information page and from ProPublica, and the wiki cannot yet capture
  PDFs.
- **"Tiny home" characterization unverified.** The Village capture describes permanent housing and
  60 Phase I units; it does not use the phrase "tiny home."
- **Therapeutic community evidence base — uncited on both sides.** The organization cites 70 years of
  research on the model. This wiki has captured no review of that literature, so it can neither
  support nor discount the model. A Cochrane or NIDA review captured as a `Type: source` page is what
  would let this page weigh the approach at all. The one methodological point that needs no citation:
  a provider's own recidivism and sobriety rates depend entirely on who is counted in the
  denominator, and this wiki has not seen that definition for any figure the Academy publishes.
