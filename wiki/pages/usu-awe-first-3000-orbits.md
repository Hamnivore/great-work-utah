## Summary

Utah State University's article documents the AWE mission's first public data release and gives the clearest institutional account of USU and Space Dynamics Laboratory responsibilities. It closely parallels NASA's March 14 account while adding USU-specific context and named project contacts.

## Useful Claims

- USU and NASA researchers released AWE data from the instrument's first 3,000 International Space Station orbits.
- The release contained more than five million nighttime-airglow and atmospheric-gravity-wave images, along with derived temperature and intensity products.
- Ludger Scherliess was the mission principal investigator and a USU physics professor.
- USU researchers and students developed software needed to process, align, screen, and publish the four telescopes' observations.
- USU led the mission; SDL built the instrument and provided the mission operations center; NASA Goddard's Explorers Program Office managed it.

## Reliability Notes

This is a primary institutional source for USU personnel, software work, leadership, and SDL's instrument and operations roles. It is not independent assessment of the mission's novelty or eventual scientific impact. The release counts and mission structure are independently consistent with NASA's official article published three days earlier.
