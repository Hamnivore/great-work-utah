## Summary

Utah Film Center's Fiscal Sponsorship program lets approved independent film projects receive charitable donations and pursue grants that require a nonprofit sponsor without forming their own 501(c)(3). Despite the Utah name, neither the filmmaker nor the project has to be based in Utah, and the official site says there is no project-budget restriction.
## Who It's For

The clearest fit is an independent filmmaker with a non-commercial project and a concrete fundraising plan who needs to accept tax-deductible donations or apply to a funder that requires nonprofit status. The program invites emerging and established filmmakers and lists documentaries, narrative films, shorts, episodic work, and other film-related artistic endeavors as possible fits.

Approval is not automatic. The Fiscal Sponsorship Committee evaluates whether a project fits Utah Film Center's mission and standards. Every project requires its own approval, including a new project from a returning filmmaker. For work outside film, contact the program before preparing a full application; the FAQ says it sponsors some other kinds of projects but does not publish their eligibility boundaries.

## How To Use It

1. Review the official [application guide](https://fiscal.utahfilmcenter.org/application-guide/) and prepare a logline, synopsis, topic context, artistic approach, production timeline, distribution and marketing plan, intended audience, engagement or social-impact plan, key-team biographies, fundraising strategy, funding raised to date, and a line-item budget. Work samples are optional. Narrative projects must also email a shooting script of no more than 20 pages to the address listed on the application page.
2. Submit the official Formsite application: https://fs28.formsite.com/RlFF1h/form2/index.html
3. Pay the $50 application fee at the end of the form. The official application guide says returning filmmakers do not pay this fee, although each new project still needs committee approval.
4. If approved, sign the program's legally binding agreement and complete its points-of-contact, banking, and W-9 onboarding documents. The individual or company producing the project manages the granted funds and remains responsible for reporting them for tax purposes.
5. Notify Utah Film Center before applying for a grant through the sponsorship so staff can check for conflicts. For NEA or NEH applications, the program requires complete materials at least one month before the funder's deadline.
6. Submit a grant request when funds are ready for disbursement. The FAQ says wires go out Tuesdays and Thursdays and physical checks on Fridays after onboarding is complete.

The officially published fiscal-sponsorship fee is 6% of funds received; the FAQ says local Utah artists and Artist Foundry members might qualify for a discount. Credit-card donations carry an additional 5% processing fee. Donors can also give by wire, check, or stock transfer. Confirm the applicable rate and payment method before directing a donor, since the public site does not publish the discount schedule.

## Open Questions

- The official pages conflict on review time: the application page promises notification within 3–4 weeks, the homepage says approval is typically within two weeks, and the FAQ says the committee usually responds within a few days. Applicants with a deadline should plan around the longest published window and confirm timing directly.
- What criteria does the committee apply when deciding whether a project fits Utah Film Center's mission and quality standard?
- What exact discount applies to Utah artists and Artist Foundry members, and which status or residency evidence is required?
- Which non-film artistic projects are eligible, and are their application materials or review criteria different?
- Are there minimum disbursement amounts, restrictions on already-raised funds, or additional charges for wires, checks, stock gifts, international recipients, or grant administration?
