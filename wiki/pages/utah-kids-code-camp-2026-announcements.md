## Summary

Four announcements posted to the Forge Utah Slack `#localevents` channel by Phil Gilmore between April
and July 2026, covering the 2026 Kids Code Camp: registration opening, a volunteer call, a
cancellation-and-reschedule, and an urgent second volunteer call.

They carry two things the event page does not: that the camp **moved from June 20 to August 1**, and
that it runs on volunteer labour that was, at one point, critically short.

## Useful Claims

- Registration for Kids Code Camp 2026 opened in April 2026, originally for **Saturday, June 20,
  2026**, at Neumont College of Computer Science.
- The camp was **rescheduled to August 1, 2026**. The reschedule was announced on 2026-05-18 by
  striking through the original date rather than by any explanation of cause.
- The organizer publicly described the event as **"critically short of help"** four days before the
  original June date, and issued a second volunteer call for the August date.
- Volunteers are recruited without prerequisite expertise; the organizer frames volunteering as
  suitable for new developers and college students building a résumé.
- The camp is free to attend and registration is required.

## Reliability Notes

The Forge Utah Slack is an **open community workspace**, joinable by anyone from the foundation's
homepage, and `#localevents` is a public announcement channel. These are promotional posts explicitly
asking readers to "share the flyer and spread the word" — public in every sense that matters for
citation.

What makes this `testimony` rather than `official-page` is durability. The permalink resolves only
once you are signed in, so no script can fetch it and the Internet Archive cannot snapshot it, and
Slack retention will eventually remove it. Hence no `**Retrieved:**`, no `**Archive:**`, no
`**Raw:**`, and quotes long enough to survive on their own.

These are the organizer's own words about his own event, so they carry no independent authority about
the camp's quality or turnout. What they carry is a **history the event page erases**: the published
2026 page shows August 1 as though it were always the date. Without these messages the reschedule and
the volunteer shortfall are unrecoverable, and both bear on whether the camp is robustly staffed.

The last quote is included because it is the source of a small structural fact — the camp's organizer
found his event missing from a Utah tech calendar and said so publicly, which is how the wiki learned
these two efforts are not automatically connected.
