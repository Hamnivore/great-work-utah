## Summary

The Utah Office of Primary Care and Rural Health (PCRH) is the Utah Department of Health and Human Services office focused on access to primary, behavioral, and oral healthcare in rural and underserved communities. It helps clinics, hospitals, communities, and healthcare professionals strengthen access; it is not itself a walk-in clinic or a substitute for emergency care.
## Who It's For

- Rural hospitals and clinics seeking technical assistance, certification guidance, or program support.
- Public and nonprofit organizations providing primary care to Utahns who are uninsured, underinsured, or earn low wages and want to assess grant eligibility.
- Clinicians and employers exploring rural recruitment, National Health Service Corps programs, state loan repayment, or the Conrad J-1 visa-waiver pathway.
- Community leaders and researchers looking for shortage designations, rural-health data, or Utah health-workforce information.

## How To Use It

- Begin with the program directory at https://ruralhealth.utah.gov/ and choose the relevant path: grants, workforce and loan repayment, rural hospitals, rural health clinics, oral health, or shortage designations.
- Clinics seeking Rural Health Clinic status can review the certification process and Utah contacts at https://ruralhealth.utah.gov/rural-health-clinics/.
- Organizations serving low-income, uninsured, or underinsured patients can review the Utah Primary Care Grants Program at https://ruralhealth.utah.gov/utah-primary-care-grant-program/. Application cycles are time-bound; verify the current cycle before planning around an award.
- Communities or providers dealing with recruitment and access shortages can start with the Primary Care Office at https://ruralhealth.utah.gov/primary-care-office/.
- People seeking care should use the site's provider and program links or contact PCRH for navigation; PCRH's published role is coordination and technical assistance rather than direct clinical service.
