## Summary

Utah State Surplus Property is the state program that disposes of state-owned vehicles, equipment, furniture, and other personal property. Availability changes continuously, so this page is a route into the live listings rather than a catalog of items.
## Who It's For

- Members of the public looking for used state-owned vehicles, equipment, furniture, or other listed inventory.
- Businesses and organizations willing to inspect listing details, accept used property without a warranty, and manage payment and collection on the state's timetable.
- Utah public agencies seeking property through the priority reuse process.

This page concerns **state-owned** surplus property. Utah's Federal Surplus Property Program is different: its ordinary recipients are eligible state and local governments and qualifying nonprofit organizations, and the governing state rule says federal surplus items are not generally available for sale to the public. An occasional federally authorized public auction does not make the federal program a standing public-purchase route.

## How To Use It

1. Start at the [official State Surplus Property page](https://purchasing.utah.gov/general-services/state-surplus-property/) and follow “View all items, auction opportunities and inventory currently available.” The inventory system also exposes dedicated vehicle, heavy-equipment, online-auction, and special-bid views.
2. For an auction, register with an email address and read the terms attached to the lot before bidding. The official auction terms limit participation to people at least 18 years old who can enter a binding contract; a winning bid is a contract to purchase.
3. Inspect the photographs, description, location, and removal requirements for the specific item. The auction terms say items are sold **as-is, where-is**, that condition is untested or unspecified, and that no warranty is expressed or implied. The inventory interface also warns that some category images are generic and may not depict the actual item.
4. Treat payment and pickup as part of the purchase decision. Online-auction terms require full payment within five business days after an auction ends, while the particular listing or invoice controls the collection instructions. The state says buyers pay shipping charges and that it does not ship outside the United States.
5. Recheck the current payment notice before paying. The program's main page and auction site have carried differing cash-payment limits, and this wiki has no captured source page preserving the current rule. Taxes, card fees, exemption documentation, and acceptable non-cash methods should be confirmed against the current payment terms and the buyer's invoice.

Public access comes after agency priority. Utah Administrative Rule R33-126 allows the state to hold property that is unique, scarce, or in high demand by public agencies for up to 30 days before offering it to the general public. A missing item is therefore not necessarily evidence that the program never receives that category.
