## Summary

The Utah Tech Calendar's explanation of **how its index is built**. This is the substantive page on
the site: it names the upstream sources, describes a four-stage pipeline from scraping to publication,
and — unusually for an aggregator — **publishes its inclusion policy with worked examples of what it
rejects**.

It is the source for the site's own operating statistics and for the editorial judgment behind them.

## Useful Claims

- Stated operating figures: **72 sources watched**, **9 curated verticals**, **4 delivery channels**,
  **0 accounts required**.
- Named upstream sources include **Meetup, Luma, Silicon Slopes, Eventbrite, 47G, BioUtah, Substack**,
  and direct **submissions** — so coverage extends past software into aerospace and life sciences.
- The pipeline runs in **four stages**: sources; **refinement** (removing certification spam and
  non-tech craft events, deduplicating, flagging webinars, sunsetting past placeholders);
  **ontology** (one schema, verticals tagged, regions placed, formats labeled); and a single
  **schedule**.
- **The inclusion policy is published with examples.** Rejected: "PMP 4 Days Classroom Training"
  (certification spam), "Woodturning: Intro to Lathe Basics" (craft, not tech), and a "Cyber Security
  1-Day Workshop" cross-posted across six cities (cross-post spam). Accepted: a UtahJS meetup, a
  Wilson Sonsini medical-device conference, and a recurring AI Power Hour at Silicon Slopes.
- The nine verticals each have their own page: **AI, fintech, healthtech, biotech, edtech, aerospace
  and defense, game dev, cybersecurity, and founders**.
- Four ways to follow it: **iCal subscription** (updating as the scraper runs), a **weekly email
  digest** filtered to chosen tags, **RSS**, and a **shareable filter URL** requiring no signup on the
  recipient's end.
- The site states it runs **no accounts, no tracking pixels or behavioral profiling, and no tracking
  cookies**.
- It claims **"+ 170 MORE THIS SEASON"** beyond the sample week shown.

## Reliability Notes

Self-reported tier, and written in a product-marketing register — the statistics are displayed as
selling points and none of them can be checked from outside.

Two internal inconsistencies are worth recording rather than smoothing over:

- **"72 sources watched" and "One schema across thirteen sources" appear on the same page.** Nothing
  reconciles them. The likely reading is that 72 counts individual watched calendars and feeds while
  thirteen counts source *types* or platforms, but the page does not say so, and neither figure should
  be quoted as authoritative without that caveat.
- **The "+170 more this season" figure has no defined period.** "Season" is undefined, and the
  homepage's 149-entry August count is a different measure over a different window.

The inclusion policy is the most valuable content here and the most carefully hedged. It is a
**statement of intent illustrated by six chosen examples**, not an audit: the examples were selected
by the operator to show the rule working. What it does establish is that filtering is deliberate and
opinionated — certification mills and non-technical craft classes are excluded on purpose — which is a
real editorial position and materially different from an unfiltered feed.

The privacy claims ("no pixels, no behavioral profile") are the site's assertions about its own
behavior and were not verified against what the page actually loads.
