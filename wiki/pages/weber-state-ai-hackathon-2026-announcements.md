## Summary

Four messages posted to the Forge Utah Slack between February and March 2026 by **Tristan Rhodes**,
an AI Integration and Automation Engineer at Weber State University, promoting the 2026 Weber State
AI Hackathon: a full invitation with format and pricing, a registration-deadline reminder, a separate
call to employers about the job fair, and a request for a speaker.

**These are the only located record of that edition.** By the time the event's own website was
captured it had already been rewritten for the following year, erasing every prior detail — the dates,
the price, the prize pool, the venue, and the two-week remote structure survive here and nowhere else.

## Useful Claims

- The 2026 hackathon ran in **two phases**: a **remote build from March 21 to April 3, 2026** with
  mentorship and tools provided, then an **in-person finale on Saturday, April 4, 2026**.
- The finale was held at **Shepherd Union, Weber State University, Ogden, Utah**.
- The ticket cost **$10**, and included **breakfast, lunch, snacks, and a t-shirt**.
- The **prize pool was $1,800**, split across **Novice, Intermediate, and Advanced** categories.
  Prizes were deliberately weighted toward the upper categories partway through, to draw experienced
  builders.
- **Registration closed March 18, 2026**, about two weeks before the finale.
- A **tech job fair ran alongside the finale, free for employers**.
- The event was **open to anyone over 18** — students, professionals, and hobbyists alike — and
  advertised as requiring **no experience**, with free AI tools and mentors provided for the two-week
  build.
- The organizer solicited a speaker on marketing and monetizing apps for the April 4 finale, so at
  least part of the program was assembled by open call.

## Reliability Notes

The Forge Utah Slack is an open community workspace and these were posted to public announcement
channels explicitly asking readers to forward them, so they are public in every sense that matters for
citation.

`testimony` rather than `official-page` because of **durability**: the permalinks resolve only for
signed-in members, nothing can fetch them signed out, the Internet Archive cannot crawl past the
login, and Slack retention will eventually delete them. Hence no `**Retrieved:**` and no
`**Archive:**`.

These are the organizer's own promotional posts, which means they establish **what was advertised and
scheduled — never what happened**. Nothing here shows how many registered, how many finished, what was
built, or whether the $1,800 was awarded. The one message posted after the finale date is a speaker
request from before it, not a report.

This is the second worked example in this corpus of the pattern where **the ephemeral channel is more
accurate than the public page**: `hackathon.weber.edu` is a live, official, first-party URL that today
tells a reader the event is in March 2027 and says essentially nothing else. A reader who trusted only
the durable source would conclude the 2026 edition never existed. The captured Slack messages are the
more complete record precisely because the website overwrote itself.

One internal caution: the prize-weighting claim comes from the March reminder describing a change made
after the February invitation, so the $1,800 total and its split across categories are stated at
different times and may not describe the same final structure.
