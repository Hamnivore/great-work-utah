## Summary

Williams International is a privately held, vertically integrated manufacturer of small gas turbine engines, founded as Williams Research Corporation in Michigan in 1954–55 by Dr. Sam B. Williams and renamed Williams International in 1981. Its Ogden, Utah plant, at 3450 Sam Williams Drive, has operated since 1978. As of the company's most recent public state filing (2025), it employs more than 900 people in Ogden and is undergoing an expansion: roughly 100,000 additional square feet of production space, 316 new high-wage jobs, and a total capital investment the state and Ogden City both describe as "over $1 billion" in Utah operations over the coming years — backed by the maximum available 30% state Economic Development Tax Increment Financing (EDTIF) credit and an Ogden City Redevelopment Agency incentive package.

For the wiki, Williams International is a straightforward navigation gap: a company with a 900+-employee Ogden manufacturing site and a state-flagged nine-figure-to-billion-dollar Utah expansion had no page at all. It is not a startup — it is a 70-year-old, founder-descended private manufacturer with an established, decades-old product line — but it is durable, real, and currently hiring at scale in a Utah city (Ogden) that gets comparatively little attention next to the Wasatch Front's Salt Lake- and Hill-centered defense cluster.

## Impact

Williams' product line spans a striking lineage: the company's small F107/F415-family turbofans power long-range cruise missiles — the Air Force's AGM-86 ALCM and, via the related F415, the Navy's Tactical Tomahawk (BGM-109) — and the same small-turbofan engineering was later adapted into the FJ33/FJ44 commercial fanjet family that powers a large share of the light and very-light business jet fleet (Cessna Citation, Cirrus Vision Jet, Pilatus PC-24, among others). The state's own economic-development citation for the 2025 incentive award states Williams has "produced over 27,000 military engines, and over 8,000 commercial engines with over 20 million hours of commercial fleet operation."

The Ogden expansion is a concrete, publicly cited bet on Utah rather than a speculative one: Ogden City's own incentive-agreement materials put the capital schedule at $366 million by 2030 plus roughly $45 million per year afterward (a total exceeding $1 billion), and both the state and city describe the incentive as necessary to keep the expansion in Utah rather than see it go to competing sites the company was evaluating in Louisiana, Texas, or Florida, citing Utah's comparatively higher labor and operating costs and an Ogden site electrical-infrastructure constraint. The honest bound on this impact: this is durable precision manufacturing and sustaining engineering for an established product family, not frontier propulsion R&D in progress — the "great work" case here rests on scarce domestic small-turbofan manufacturing capability and several hundred high-wage production jobs, not a technology breakthrough.

## Utah Context

Williams International sits in Ogden's Weber County aerospace-manufacturing corridor, a few miles from [Hill Air Force Base](hill-air-force-base.md)'s Ogden Air Logistics Complex, though available public sourcing does not establish Williams as a Hill prime contractor — the two are best understood as separate anchors of the same regional aerospace-manufacturing labor market rather than as a supply-chain pair. It is a much smaller and more narrowly focused manufacturer than [Northrop Grumman's Promontory facility](northrop-grumman-promontory.md) (solid rocket motors, roughly 5,000+ employees) but plays a comparable role for Ogden that Promontory plays for Box Elder County: a decades-old, single-site aerospace employer that does not require relocating to a coastal aerospace hub. [47G](47g-utah-aerospace-and-defense-association.md), the statewide aerospace/defense association, is the natural ecosystem connector for a manufacturer at this scale.

## What They Need Now

Williams' public materials point to durable role families rather than a churning list of current openings. The company maintains standing, location-specific recruiting pages for CNC machinist and precision-manufacturing/production roles at its Ogden site specifically, consistent with Ogden City's own description of the expansion as adding "primarily high-wage, skilled production occupations," supported by company-run job training. On the engineering side, Williams runs an Engineering Rotational Program for new bachelor's/master's graduates that rotates hires through mechanical design, structural and thermal analysis, aerodynamics, thermodynamic cycle modeling, vibration/rotordynamics, electrical/electronics, control systems, software development, manufacturing engineering, and engine test — the clearest public signal of the breadth of engineering work behind the company's small-turbine product line — alongside a standing internship program. A 100,000-square-foot facility expansion tied to an electrical-infrastructure upgrade also implies ongoing demand for facilities, project, and quality engineering, though the company does not itemize these publicly.

## Who Could Help

Useful helpers include Ogden-area workforce-training partners (the state and city incentive package earmarks dedicated job-creation and job-training funding), Utah Department of Workforce Services placement staff, Weber County and Ogden City economic-development offices already engaged on the expansion, and veteran-hiring pipelines — Williams is separately recognized by both the Michigan Veterans Affairs Agency and the Utah Patriot Partnership for veteran recruiting and retention.

## Open Questions

- Williams' own website does not connect its current business to its documented military/cruise-missile engine history anywhere found on the current site; that connection here rests on Utah/Ogden government economic-development documents, Wikipedia, and a Michigan environmental-permit inspection record rather than the company's current self-description — a stronger source would be a company-published military-programs page, if one exists but was not found.
- Neither the company nor available government sourcing states what fraction of the roughly 900 current Ogden employees (or the 316 planned new positions) are engineering versus production/skilled-trades roles.
- News coverage of the expansion (ABC4, February 2025) states that "the timeline of the company's expansion, and when jobs might open for applications, is not immediately clear" — public sourcing on expansion pacing may now be dated given the incentive was finalized in early-to-mid 2025.
- Is the Ogden site a full design-to-production facility, as one third-party business-profile aggregator claims, or primarily manufacturing/final-assembly with design centered at the Michigan headquarters? Williams' own site does not break this out by location.
- Ownership structure beyond "privately owned" is not independently confirmed here — the company is run by chairman/president/CEO Gregg G. Williams, but this page does not assert a specific founder-family-ownership claim without stronger sourcing.
- Confirm current status of the EDTIF-/TIF-funded electrical-infrastructure upgrade, which both the state and city describe as a gating item for the expansion to proceed.
